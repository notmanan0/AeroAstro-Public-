# Ideal-gas Law

The ideal-gas law is the equation of state $pV=mRT$, equivalently $pv=RT$ or $p=\rho RT$, with $p$ and $T$ absolute and $R$ the specific gas constant of the gas.

It follows from kinetic theory for molecules that occupy negligible volume and do not interact except in collisions: pressure is the momentum flux of molecules hitting the wall, and temperature measures their mean kinetic energy. Per mole it reads $pV=nR_uT$ with the universal constant $R_u=8.3145$ J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹; dividing by the molar mass gives the specific constant $R=R_u/M$, 287 J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹ for air.

The law ties the three properties together, so any two fix the third and, with the state postulate, the whole state of a simple gas. For a fixed mass going between two states it gives the useful ratio form $p_1V_1/T_1=p_2V_2/T_2$, in which $R$ cancels.

Two traps recur in exam questions. Temperatures must be in kelvin and pressures absolute: a tyre gauge reading or a Celsius temperature put straight into the law gives nonsense. And the law describes gases well only far from condensation, at low pressure and high temperature relative to the critical point; steam near saturation or a gas at very high pressure needs tables or a correction.
