# Band Theory and Semiconductor Doping

## Definition

> [!note] Definition
> - In a solid, atomic energy levels broaden into **bands**. Conduction needs electrons in a part-filled band.
> - A **semiconductor** has a full valence band and an empty conduction band separated by a **narrow gap** (Si: 1.1 eV), so heat promotes a few electrons, leaving mobile **holes**.
> - **Doping** adds carriers: **n-type** (group V donors such as P, extra electrons) or **p-type** (group III acceptors such as B, extra holes).

## Explanation
- **Conductor**: the bands overlap. **Insulator**: the gap is too wide.
- A hole behaves as a positive carrier that drifts towards the negative terminal as electrons hop into it.
- **Majority** carriers come from doping; **minority** carriers come from thermal generation. The minority population is why semiconductor devices are temperature-dependent.

## Examples
- An NTC thermistor's resistance falls ~4 %/°C as more carriers are thermally generated.
- A photodiode or solar cell uses photons with energy above the gap to create electron–hole pairs.
