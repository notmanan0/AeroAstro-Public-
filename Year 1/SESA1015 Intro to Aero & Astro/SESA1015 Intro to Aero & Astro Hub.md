# SESA1015 Intro to Aero & Astro

> [!abstract] Module map
> SESA1015 has three strands. Mechanics of Flight (Prof. Andy Keane) builds aircraft performance from the atmosphere and the aerodynamic coefficients up to range, take-off, turning, trim and preliminary sizing; it feeds most of Year 2 and is the priority. Astronautics covers missions, the space and launch environments, the rocket equation, staging and launch vehicles. Aircraft Operations is filed under Sources and is not yet written up.

The Mechanics of Flight notes follow the course-notes PDF section by section, with the slides (version 2) supplying the notation and the worked examples. Every topic note derives its results in full, states where each assumption enters, and ends with self-study tasks drawn from the 42-question revision workbook, whose full solutions are in the T-notes below. Keep [[SESA1015 Formula Sheet]] open while working problems.

All topic notes have status `lectured`: the module has been taught, but the transcripts were deleted, so the notes are written from the course notes, slides, spreadsheets and workbook.

## Exam skills

Most Mechanics of Flight questions are short numerical chains: convert mass to weight, find the density for the altitude, use lift balance to get $C_L$, then apply the right optimum ($C_L=\sqrt{C_{D0}/K}$ for minimum drag and propeller range, $\sqrt{3C_{D0}/K}$ for minimum power, $\sqrt{C_{D0}/(3K)}$ for jet range at constant altitude) and check the speed against stall. The take-off, turn and trim questions add one physical idea each (friction on the unloaded weight, $n=1/\cos\phi$, moment balance about the CG). Astronautics questions are mostly the rocket equation with mass fractions, plus descriptive questions on the environments.
