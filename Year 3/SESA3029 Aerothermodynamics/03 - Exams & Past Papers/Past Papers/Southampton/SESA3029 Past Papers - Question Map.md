# SESA3029 Past Papers - Question Map

The 12 Southampton SESA3029 papers from 2014–15 to 2024–25 hold 51 questions; 2019–20 and 2020–21 were online take-home papers, and 2024–25 has a closed-book Paper A and an open-book Paper B. Coverage is as of 19 Nov 2026. The compressible-flow material of Weeks 1–8 (shocks, nozzles, the Method of Characteristics, Prandtl–Glauert and Ackeret theory) has been lectured and has topic notes. Heat transfer (Weeks 9–12) has not been lectured yet, but its topic notes (`W09a`–`W09f`) are now written ahead from the lecture slides, and its questions are solved in full in each paper's note.

## Repeat and near-repeat clusters

### R1. Supersonic wind tunnel and Laval nozzle

2014–15 Q1(i), 2016–17 Q1(i–ii), 2017–18 Q1(i), 2018–19 Q1(i–iii), 2019–20 Q1(i), 2021–22 Q1(ii) and Q2(ii), 2023–24 Q1(i) and 2024–25 A/B Q1(i). The method is the same every year: read $p/p_0$, $T/T_0$ and $A/A^*$ from the IFT at the test-section Mach number to recover the reservoir and throat states, and, when the exit pressure differs from the back pressure, find the oblique shock or expansion fan at the nozzle exit.

### R2. Shock-expansion against Ackeret theory on supersonic aerofoils

This appears in almost every paper: 2014–15 Q1(ii–iii), 2015–16 Q2, 2016–17 Q2 (Ackeret derivation and a parabolic arc), 2017–18 Q1(ii–v), 2018–19 Q1(iv–v) and Q2, 2019–20 Q1(ii–v), 2020–21 Q1, 2021–22 Q1(v–vi), 2022–23 Q1 and 2024–25 A/B Q1(iii–v). The Ackeret derivations in 2016–17 Q2(i) and 2018–19 Q2(i) are the same question, and the 2022–23 Q1 triangular section is close to the 2024–25 A/B Q1 half-diamond.

### R3. Prandtl–Glauert derivation and critical Mach number

A 14–16 mark question in 2015–16 Q1(ii, iv), 2017–18 Q2, 2019–20 Q2, 2021–22 Q1(iv), 2022–23 Q2 and 2024–25 A/B Q2. The two 2024–25 papers ask the same question ($C_{p,\min}=-1.2$); 2022–23 uses the same wording with $C_{p0,\min}=-5.4$. The second part either gives $C_{p,\min,0}$ and asks for $M_{\text{crit}}$, found by trial or graphically, or gives $M_{\text{crit}}$ and asks for $C_{p,\min,0}$ (2017–18).

### R4. Oblique shock reflection from a wall and SWBLI

2014–15 Q2(ii–iii), 2015–16 Q3(i–ii) (reflection at a convex corner), 2016–17 Q1(iii–vii), 2021–22 Q1(vii), 2023–24 Q2 and 2024–25 A/B Q1(ii). 2014–15 Q2 and 2016–17 Q1 are near-twins covering regular reflection, the switch to a Mach stem when the required turn exceeds $\theta_{\max}(M_2)$, and shock-wave/boundary-layer interaction.

### R5. Method of Characteristics and minimum-length nozzles

Three groups repeat. 2014–15 Q3(i) ($M_e=3.6$) and 2017–18 Q3 ($M_e=3.0$) both find interior point 8 from points 4 and 7 with $M_4=M_7$. 2016–17 Q3, 2018–19 Q3 and 2019–20 Q3 are the two-wave prototype nozzle with $\theta_{\max}=\nu(M_e)/2$ and points 1–4, and 2022–23 Q3 is close to them. 2024–25 A Q3 and B Q3 are the same jet-boundary question for an under-expanded nozzle.

### R6. Finite-difference conduction stencils

Heat transfer (Weeks 9–12, written ahead in [[SESA3029 W09e - Numerical Methods in Conduction|W09e]], not yet lectured). 2019–20 Q4 and 2020–21 Q3 share the format of a 2D heated plate with a convective boundary. 2022–23 Q4 is a 2D symmetric block. 2024–25 A Q5 and B Q5(i) are the same internal-corner stencil with a heat-flux boundary, and 2024–25 B Q5(ii) repeats 2023–24 Q1(ix), the 1D radial cylindrical stencil.

### R7. 1D Euler characteristic speeds and nozzle CFD boundary conditions

2016–17 Q4(iii) and 2017–18 Q5(i) ask for the same flux-Jacobian eigenvalues $u$ and $u\pm a$ of the 1D isentropic Euler equations. 2014–15 Q3(iii) and 2018–19 Q5 test characteristic inflow and outflow boundary conditions in nozzle CFD.

### R8. Dimensionless groups, flat-plate convection and re-entry radiation

Heat transfer (Weeks 9–12, written ahead in [[SESA3029 W09b - Convective Heat Transfer and the Reynolds Analogy|W09b]] and [[SESA3029 W09d - Thermal Radiation|W09d]], not yet lectured). 2014–15 Q4, 2015–16 Q4 and 2017–18 Q4 are a nearly word-for-word sequence on Nu, Re, Pr and $C_H$, laminar flat-plate heat transfer, and shock-layer radiation in lunar re-entry. 2016–17 Q4(i–ii), 2018–19 Q4 and 2023–24 Q1(viii) test flat-plate convection (tripped, turbulent or laminar) and the Reynolds analogy.

### R9. Richardson extrapolation

Covered in [[SESA3029 W09e - Numerical Methods in Conduction#7. Order of accuracy|W09e §7]]. 2014–15 Q4(iii) ($p=2$), 2015–16 Q4(iv) ($p=3$), 2017–18 Q5(ii) ($p=2$, in a compressible-flow setting), 2019–20 Q4(iv) (order $p$ found from three grids, then extrapolated) and 2021–22 Q3(iii).

### R10. Shell-and-tube heat exchangers

Heat transfer (Weeks 9–12, written ahead in [[SESA3029 W09f - Heat Exchangers|W09f]], not yet lectured). 2022–23 Q5 sizes a counterflow exchanger by LMTD. 2023–24 Q4 uses $\varepsilon$–NTU for a condenser, where $C_r=0$. 2024–25 A Q4 and B Q4 are the same $\varepsilon$–NTU question.
