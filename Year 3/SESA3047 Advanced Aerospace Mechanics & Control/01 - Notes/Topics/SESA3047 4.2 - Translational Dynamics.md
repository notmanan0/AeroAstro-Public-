# SESA3047 4.2 - Translational Dynamics

> [!abstract] Summary
> Newton's law requires **inertial** acceleration. On rotating Earth, centrifugal acceleration can be absorbed into effective gravity, leaving a Coriolis term. Under the flat-Earth approximation, local NED is inertial and gravity is constant. Body axes still rotate, so their measured velocity derivatives differ from inertial acceleration:
>
> $$\boxed{\dot{\mathbf v}^{FRD}=\mathbf F^{FRD}/m+\mathbf C_{FRD/NED}\mathbf g^{NED}-\boldsymbol\omega^{FRD}\times\mathbf v^{FRD}.}$$
>
> Here $\mathbf F$ **excludes gravity**. These three derivatives complete the dynamics needed for the 12-state model.

## The topic in one physical picture

Imagine a sensor reporting the velocity components along the aircraft's nose, right wing and belly. If the aircraft yaws while its velocity in Earth directions stays the same, the forward and lateral readings change. That change does not need a new force: the coordinate axes have turned.

The term $-\boldsymbol\omega\times\mathbf v$ accounts for that turning. Gravity is a separate physical acceleration; its direction is simple in NED and must be rotated into FRD before adding it to body-axis forces.

## 1. Newton's law and the two meanings of gravity (4.26–4.31)

Let $\mathbf F$ contain aerodynamic, propulsive and other **non-gravitational** forces, and let $\mathbf G$ be true gravitational acceleration. For constant mass,

$$
\mathbf Q=m\mathbf v_{cm/i},
$$

$$
\frac{{}^i\mathrm d\mathbf Q}{\mathrm dt}
=m\mathbf a_{cm/i}
=\sum\mathbf F_{ext}
=\mathbf F+m\mathbf G.
$$

### Step 1: keep Earth rotation initially

Let $\boldsymbol\Omega=\boldsymbol\omega_{e/i}$ be constant and take the Earth-centred origin to have negligible inertial acceleration. The moving-frame acceleration formula from [[SESA3047 3.2 - Translational Kinematics and Accelerations in Moving Frames|3.2]] gives

$$
\mathbf a_i=\mathbf a_e+
\boldsymbol\Omega\times(\boldsymbol\Omega\times\mathbf p)
+2\boldsymbol\Omega\times\mathbf v_e.
$$

There is no angular-acceleration term because $\dot{\boldsymbol\Omega}=\mathbf0$. Here $\mathbf p$ is measured from the **Earth's centre**, not the local NED origin.

### Step 2: substitute into Newton's law

$$
m\left[\mathbf a_e+
\boldsymbol\Omega\times(\boldsymbol\Omega\times\mathbf p)
+2\boldsymbol\Omega\times\mathbf v_e\right]
=\mathbf F+m\mathbf G,
$$

$$
\mathbf a_e=\frac{\mathbf F}{m}+\mathbf G
-\boldsymbol\Omega\times(\boldsymbol\Omega\times\mathbf p)
-2\boldsymbol\Omega\times\mathbf v_e.
$$

### Step 3: define effective gravity

$$
\mathbf g=\mathbf G-\boldsymbol\Omega\times(\boldsymbol\Omega\times\mathbf p),
$$

$$
\boxed{\mathbf a_e=\mathbf F/m+\mathbf g-2\boldsymbol\Omega\times\mathbf v_e.}
$$

> [!warning] Do not count centrifugal acceleration twice
> If $\mathbf g$ is effective gravity, the centrifugal term is already included. $\mathbf G$ denotes gravitation alone. The Coriolis term remains separate.

## 2. Apply the flat-Earth assumptions (4.32)

Treat a local NED frame as inertial, neglect Earth rotation and curvature, and take constant gravity:

$$
\boldsymbol\Omega=\mathbf0,\qquad
\mathbf a_i=\mathbf a_e,\qquad
\mathbf g=\mathbf G,\qquad
\mathbf g^{NED}=\begin{bmatrix}0\\0\\g\end{bmatrix},\quad g=9.81\ \mathrm{m/s^2}.
$$

Newton's law then becomes

$$
\mathbf a_{cm/e}=\mathbf F/m+\mathbf g.
$$

The down component is positive because NED uses **down**, not height, as its third coordinate. This approximation does **not** say that the body frame is inertial.

## 3. Derive the body-velocity equation (4.33–4.37)

### Step 1: use the transport theorem

For the Earth-relative velocity vector,

$$
{}^e\dot{\mathbf v}_{cm/e}
={}^b\dot{\mathbf v}_{cm/e}
+\boldsymbol\omega_{b/e}\times\mathbf v_{cm/e}.
$$

Under flat Earth, $\boldsymbol\omega_{b/e}=\boldsymbol\omega_{b/i}$. Expressing all components in FRD gives

$$
\mathbf a^{FRD}_{cm/e}
=\dot{\mathbf v}^{FRD}+\boldsymbol\omega^{FRD}\times\mathbf v^{FRD}.
$$

The dot on components is the body-frame derivative; it is the derivative required when integrating $u,v,w$.

### Step 2: substitute the force balance

$$
\dot{\mathbf v}^{FRD}+\boldsymbol\omega^{FRD}\times\mathbf v^{FRD}
=\mathbf F^{FRD}/m+\mathbf g^{FRD},
$$

$$
\dot{\mathbf v}^{FRD}
=\mathbf F^{FRD}/m+\mathbf g^{FRD}
-\boldsymbol\omega^{FRD}\times\mathbf v^{FRD}.
$$

### Step 3: rotate gravity

The DCM $\mathbf C=\mathbf C_{FRD/NED}$ maps **NED components into FRD**. Multiplying by $[0,0,g]^T$ selects its third column:

$$
\mathbf g^{FRD}=\mathbf C\begin{bmatrix}0\\0\\g\end{bmatrix}
=g\begin{bmatrix}
-\sin\theta\\
\sin\phi\cos\theta\\
\cos\phi\cos\theta
\end{bmatrix}.
$$

Hence Eq. (4.37) is

$$
\boxed{\dot{\mathbf v}
=\frac{\mathbf F}{m}+\mathbf C\mathbf g^{NED}
-\tilde{\boldsymbol\omega}\mathbf v.}
$$

> [!tip] Gravity gives a useful DCM check
> No yaw angle appears because yaw about local down cannot change a vertical vector. Orthogonality preserves $|\mathbf g^{FRD}|=g$. At zero bank and pitch, gravity is $[0,0,g]^T$ in FRD too.

## 4. Scalar equations and force conventions (4.38–4.40)

Expand

$$
\boldsymbol\omega\times\mathbf v
=\begin{bmatrix}p\\q\\r\end{bmatrix}
\times\begin{bmatrix}u\\v\\w\end{bmatrix}
=\begin{bmatrix}qw-rv\\ru-pw\\pv-qu\end{bmatrix}.
$$

### Forces excluding gravity

With $\mathbf F=[F_x,F_y,F_z]^T$,

$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
\dot u&=F_x/m-g\sin\theta+rv-qw,\\
\dot v&=F_y/m+g\sin\phi\cos\theta+pw-ru,\\
\dot w&=F_z/m+g\cos\phi\cos\theta+qu-pv.
\end{aligned}}
$$

### Forces including gravity

The handout switches convention before Eq. (4.38). Define

$$
\mathbf F_B=\mathbf F+m\mathbf g^{FRD}
=\begin{bmatrix}X\\Y\\Z\end{bmatrix}.
$$

Then

$$
\mathbf F_B=m(\dot{\mathbf v}+\boldsymbol\omega\times\mathbf v),
$$

$$
\boxed{X=m(\dot u+qw-rv),\quad
Y=m(\dot v+ru-pw),\quad
Z=m(\dot w+pv-qu).}
$$

> [!warning] Choose one force convention consistently
> In Eq. (4.37), $\mathbf F$ excludes weight and gravity is added explicitly. In Eq. (4.40), $X,Y,Z$ include weight. Adding another $g$ to the second convention doubles gravity.

## 5. Worked derivative check (suggested)

Choose

$$
m=1000\ \mathrm{kg},\quad
[\phi,\theta,\psi]=[20^\circ,10^\circ,30^\circ],
$$

$$
\mathbf v=[50,2,3]^T\ \mathrm{m/s},\quad
\boldsymbol\omega=[0.5,0.1,0.05]^T\ \mathrm{rad/s},
$$

$$
\mathbf F=[1000,100,-9000]^T\ \mathrm N
\quad\text{(excluding gravity)}.
$$

Rotate gravity:

$$
\mathbf g^{FRD}
=9.81[-\sin10^\circ,\ \sin20^\circ\cos10^\circ,\ \cos20^\circ\cos10^\circ]^T,
$$

$$
\mathbf g^{FRD}=[-1.70348862,\ 3.30424431,\ 9.07833663]^T\ \mathrm{m/s^2}.
$$

Find the coordinate-rotation term:

$$
\boldsymbol\omega\times\mathbf v
=\begin{bmatrix}0.1(3)-0.05(2)\\0.05(50)-0.5(3)\\0.5(2)-0.1(50)\end{bmatrix}
=\begin{bmatrix}0.2\\1\\-4\end{bmatrix}\ \mathrm{m/s^2}.
$$

Add force per mass and gravity, then subtract the cross product:

$$
\dot{\mathbf v}
=\begin{bmatrix}1\\0.1\\-9\end{bmatrix}
+\begin{bmatrix}-1.70348862\\3.30424431\\9.07833663\end{bmatrix}
-\begin{bmatrix}0.2\\1\\-4\end{bmatrix},
$$

$$
\boxed{\dot{\mathbf v}
=[-0.90348862,\ 2.40424431,\ 4.07833663]^T\ \mathrm{m/s^2}.}
$$

> [!check] Verify the convention independently
> The total force is $\mathbf F_B=[-703.48862,3404.24431,78.33663]^T$ N. Dividing by mass and subtracting the same cross product gives the same derivative. Rotating $\dot{\mathbf v}+\boldsymbol\omega\times\mathbf v$ back to NED gives $\mathbf C^T\mathbf F/m+[0,0,g]^T$.

## 6. Limiting checks

- **Level, no body rotation, free fall:** $\mathbf F=\mathbf0$ gives $\dot{\mathbf v}=[0,0,g]^T$.
- **Level supported flight:** $\mathbf F=[0,0,-mg]^T$, zero body rates, gives $\dot{\mathbf v}=\mathbf0$.
- **Turning coordinates:** with zero physical acceleration, $\dot{\mathbf v}=-\boldsymbol\omega\times\mathbf v$; body components change while Earth velocity does not.
- **Height sign:** $h=-z$ relative to the chosen NED origin, so $\dot h=-\dot z$.
