# SESA3047 4.3 - Complete Flat-Earth 6-DOF Model

> [!abstract] Summary
> Six physical degrees of freedom require **twelve first-order states** when position, velocity, Euler angles and angular velocity are integrated. The model has four groups: translational kinematics, rotational kinematics, translational dynamics and rotational dynamics. Each group must use the same current state. $\mathbf C$ transforms vector components; $\mathbf H$ transforms body rates into Euler rates. Neither creates extra degrees of freedom.

> [!info] Coverage
> Written from Chapter_4.pdf, pp. 9–10, §4.3. No matching transcript has arrived. The force and moment laws required to close the model are inputs to this chapter; aerodynamic modelling, trim, linearisation and control design are not yet covered here.

## The topic in one physical picture

Forces change velocity; velocity changes position. Moments change angular velocity; angular velocity changes attitude. Attitude then changes gravity components and the transformation from body velocity to ground track. The two motions are coupled through the current state even when the model has no controller.

A body can yaw during free fall while its centre of mass follows a simple parabola. The worked check below uses that situation to verify the entire chain of transformations and derivatives.

## 1. State order and assumptions (4.41)

Use the handout's state order:

$$
\boxed{\mathbf x=
[x,y,z,\ \phi,\theta,\psi,\ u,v,w,\ p,q,r]^T.}
$$

| Group | Coordinates | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| $x,y,z$ | NED | position from fixed local origin $Q$ |
| $\phi,\theta,\psi$ | 3-2-1 attitude | roll, pitch, yaw |
| $u,v,w$ | FRD | Earth-relative velocity components |
| $p,q,r$ | FRD | angular velocity relative to Earth; also inertial under flat Earth |

Position is a vector $\mathbf p$, while scalar $p$ is a roll rate. Keep them distinct.

Assumptions: rigid body, constant mass and body-frame inertia; local NED treated as inertial; constant $g$; no Earth curvature or rotation; Euler angles away from $\cos\theta=0$. Internal rotors require the extension in [[SESA3047 4.1 - Inertia Models and Rotor Dynamics#5. Spinning internal rotors (4.15–4.25)|4.1 §5]].

## 2. Assemble the four equation groups (4.42)

Define $\mathbf C=\mathbf C_{FRD/NED}(\phi,\theta,\psi)$, $\boldsymbol\Phi=[\phi,\theta,\psi]^T$, $\mathbf v=[u,v,w]^T$, $\boldsymbol\omega=[p,q,r]^T$ and $\mathbf g^{NED}=[0,0,g]^T$. $\mathbf F$ excludes gravity.

$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\dot{\mathbf p}^{NED}&=\mathbf C^T\mathbf v,\\
\dot{\boldsymbol\Phi}&=\mathbf H(\boldsymbol\Phi)\boldsymbol\omega,\\
\dot{\mathbf v}&=\mathbf F/m+\mathbf C\mathbf g^{NED}-\tilde{\boldsymbol\omega}\mathbf v,\\
\dot{\boldsymbol\omega}&=\mathbf J^{-1}(\mathbf M-\tilde{\boldsymbol\omega}\mathbf J\boldsymbol\omega).
\end{aligned}}
$$

The first two groups are kinematics; the last two are dynamics. Derivations: [[SESA3047 3.2 - Translational Kinematics and Accelerations in Moving Frames|3.2]], [[SESA3047 3.1 - Rotational Kinematics and Euler-Angle Rates|3.1]], [[SESA3047 4.2 - Translational Dynamics|4.2]], and [[SESA3047 3.3 - Rigid-Body Rotational Dynamics|3.3]].

### Position: why the transpose is needed

Since $\mathbf v^{FRD}=\mathbf C\mathbf v^{NED}$,

$$
\mathbf v^{NED}=\mathbf C^{-1}\mathbf v^{FRD}
=\mathbf C^T\mathbf v^{FRD}
=\dot{\mathbf p}^{NED}.
$$

Writing $c_\alpha=\cos\alpha$, $s_\alpha=\sin\alpha$ gives the full rows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\dot x={}&c_\theta c_\psi u
+(s_\phi s_\theta c_\psi-c_\phi s_\psi)v
+(c_\phi s_\theta c_\psi+s_\phi s_\psi)w,\\
\dot y={}&c_\theta s_\psi u
+(s_\phi s_\theta s_\psi+c_\phi c_\psi)v
+(c_\phi s_\theta s_\psi-s_\phi c_\psi)w,\\
\dot z={}&-s_\theta u+s_\phi c_\theta v+c_\phi c_\theta w.
\end{aligned}
$$

### Attitude: why body rates are not Euler rates

$$
\begin{aligned}
\dot\phi&=p+(q\sin\phi+r\cos\phi)\tan\theta,\\
\dot\theta&=q\cos\phi-r\sin\phi,\\
\dot\psi&=(q\sin\phi+r\cos\phi)/\cos\theta.
\end{aligned}
$$

$\mathbf H$ differs from $\mathbf C$: its columns combine rates about successive Euler axes. Its singularity is a coordinate limitation, not infinite physical angular velocity.

### Velocity and angular velocity

Use the scalar velocity equations from [[SESA3047 4.2 - Translational Dynamics#4. Scalar equations and force conventions (4.38–4.40)|4.2 §4]]. For angular velocity use the matrix solve for any inertia matrix, Euler's equations only for principal axes, or the aircraft form when its symmetry assumptions hold.

For rotor momentum, replace only the rotational dynamics group:

$$
\dot{\boldsymbol\omega}=\mathbf J^{-1}
\left(\mathbf M-\boldsymbol\omega\times\mathbf J\boldsymbol\omega
-\boldsymbol\omega\times\mathbf h_R-{}^b\dot{\mathbf h}_R\right).
$$

Rotor spin dynamics may require extra states if rotor speeds are not prescribed.

## 3. Evaluate the derivative for a numerical solver

The system is closed only after supplying $\mathbf F(\mathbf x,\boldsymbol\delta,t)$ and $\mathbf M(\mathbf x,\boldsymbol\delta,t)$, where $\boldsymbol\delta$ represents control inputs.

At each right-hand-side evaluation:

1. Unpack the **current trial state**, using the state order in §1.
2. Calculate $\mathbf C$ and $\mathbf H$ from the current attitude.
3. Calculate current forces and moments, including controls and any state dependence.
4. Evaluate all four derivative groups.
5. Return one 12-component vector in the original state order.

An adaptive solver such as MATLAB ode45 evaluates this function several times per step. Recalculate the state-dependent quantities at each evaluation; do not reuse attitude or loads from a previous accepted step.

    position_dot = C.T @ velocity
    attitude_dot = H @ omega
    velocity_dot = force / mass + C @ gravity_ned - cross(omega, velocity)
    omega_dot = solve(J, moment - cross(omega, J @ omega))
    state_dot = concatenate([position_dot, attitude_dot, velocity_dot, omega_dot])

Initial conditions are needed for all twelve states. The DCM is evaluated algebraically from the Euler angles; it is not an additional differential equation in this representation.

## 4. Worked full-state derivative (suggested)

Combine the translational example in 4.2 with the aircraft example in 4.1:

$$
[\phi,\theta,\psi]=[20^\circ,10^\circ,30^\circ],\quad
\mathbf v=[50,2,3]^T,\quad
\boldsymbol\omega=[0.5,0.1,0.05]^T,
$$

$$
m=1000,\quad
\mathbf J=\begin{bmatrix}1285&0&-100\\0&1825&0\\-100&0&2667\end{bmatrix},
$$

$$
\mathbf F=[1000,100,-9000]^T,\qquad
\mathbf M=[500,0,0]^T.
$$

Use SI units and convert angles to radians before evaluating trigonometric functions. Initial position is arbitrary for this derivative because these chosen loads do not depend on it.

$$
\mathbf C^T\mathbf v=
[42.74357618,\ 25.66334983,\ -5.23251097]^T\ \mathrm{m/s},
$$

$$
\mathbf H\boldsymbol\omega=
[0.51431540,\ 0.07686825,\ 0.08243908]^T\ \mathrm{rad/s},
$$

$$
\dot{\mathbf v}=
[-0.90348862,\ 2.40424431,\ 4.07833663]^T\ \mathrm{m/s^2},
$$

$$
\dot{\boldsymbol\omega}=
[0.39005557,\ 0.00536986,\ 0.00431404]^T\ \mathrm{rad/s^2}.
$$

> [!check] Read the signs physically
> Negative $\dot z$ means height is increasing at 5.23251 m/s. It does not imply that body-axis $w$ is negative: the attitude determines how each body component projects onto NED.

## 5. Exact ballistic integration check (suggested)

![[amc_6dof_ballistic_trajectory.png|850]]

Take a freely falling body with $\mathbf F=\mathbf M=\mathbf0$, $\mathbf J=\operatorname{diag}(2,3,4)$ kg m² and initial state

$$
\mathbf x(0)=[0,0,-1000,\ 0,0,0,\ 50,0,0,\ 0,0,0.3]^T.
$$

There is zero **non-gravitational** force; weight still acts. Pure rotation about the principal $z$ axis remains pure rotation:

$$
p=q=0,\quad r=0.3,\quad \phi=\theta=0,\quad\psi=0.3t.
$$

The NED acceleration is $[0,0,g]^T$. Integrate once and then again:

$$
\dot x=50,\quad\dot y=0,\quad\dot z=gt,
$$

$$
x=50t,\quad y=0,\quad z=-1000+\tfrac12gt^2.
$$

Rotate that velocity into body axes:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}u\\v\\w\end{bmatrix}
=\begin{bmatrix}
\cos\psi&\sin\psi&0\\
-\sin\psi&\cos\psi&0\\
0&0&1
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}50\\0\\gt\end{bmatrix},
$$

$$
u=50\cos(0.3t),\qquad v=-50\sin(0.3t),\qquad w=gt.
$$

Differentiate to verify the dynamics directly:

$$
\dot u=-15\sin(0.3t)=rv,\quad
\dot v=-15\cos(0.3t)=-ru,\quad
\dot w=g.
$$

![[amc_6dof_body_velocity_check.png|850]]

At $t=10$ s:

| Quantity | Exact value |
|---|---:|
| north displacement $x$ | 500 m |
| east displacement $y$ | 0 m |
| height $-z$ | 509.5 m |
| yaw $\psi$ | 3 rad |
| $u$ | −49.49962483 m/s |
| $v$ | −7.05600040 m/s |
| $w$ | 98.1 m/s |

> [!check] All twelve states integrated
> A numerical integration over 0–10 s, with relative tolerance $10^{-10}$ and absolute tolerance $10^{-12}$, agrees with the analytic components to a maximum absolute error of $1.22\times10^{-10}$ (using each component's SI unit). The implementation asserts a looser $10^{-6}$ tolerance for portability. This checks signs, state order and coordinate transforms; it does not validate an aerodynamic force model.

## 6. Source notes and common mistakes

- Handout p. 10, §4.3.3 has an unclosed parenthesis in the general function notation. Read it as $\dot{\mathbf x}=\mathbf f(\mathbf x,\boldsymbol\delta,t)$, with force and moment laws evaluated inside the function.
- A 6-DOF model has six physical coordinates, not six first-order states. Velocities supply another six.
- $\mathbf C^T\mathbf v$ gives position rate; $\mathbf C\mathbf g^{NED}$ gives body gravity. Reversing either transform gives the wrong directions.
- The baseline Eq. (4.42) has no internal-rotor terms. Add them when modelling spinning components.
- The gravity-including force convention must not be mixed with the explicit gravity term.
- Stop before the Euler-angle singularity or change attitude representation; this chapter does not derive a quaternion model.
