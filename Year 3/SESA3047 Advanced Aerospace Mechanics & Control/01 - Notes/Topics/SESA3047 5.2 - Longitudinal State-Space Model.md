# SESA3047 5.2 - Longitudinal State-Space Model

> [!abstract] Summary
> The linearised equations of 5.1 split into a longitudinal set ($u,w,q$) and a lateral set ($v,p,r$). For the longitudinal set, the out-of-balance forces are gravity, linearised about the trim climb angle $\gamma_0$ to $-mg\cos\gamma_0\,\theta$ and $-mg\sin\gamma_0\,\theta$, plus aerodynamic terms written with aerodynamic derivatives such as $\mathring Z_w$ and $\mathring M_q$. Adding $\dot\theta=q$ as a fourth equation gives the complete $4\times4$ model, Eq. 5.18, which becomes $\dot{\mathbf x}=\mathbf A\mathbf x$ once the mass matrix on the left is inverted.

> [!summary]- Exam use: quick path
> - **Use it when:** asked to derive the longitudinal (or, as in Practice problem 3, the lateral) state-space model from the linearised equations and given force expansions.
> - **Assumptions:** trim in steady straight flight at climb angle $\gamma_0$, $x$ along $U_\infty$; small perturbations, angles in radians; symmetric aircraft, so longitudinal and lateral sets decouple; $\mathring X_q=\mathring X_{\dot w}=0$; no control inputs in Eq. 5.16.
> - **Method:** 1. Write each perturbed equation as inertia side (Eqs. 5.7–5.8) = gravity + aerodynamic out-of-balance terms. 2. Linearise gravity about $\gamma_0$; the $\epsilon^0$ part is trim and cancels. 3. Move every derivative ($\dot u,\dot w,\dot q$, including $\mathring Z_{\dot w}\dot w$, $\mathring M_{\dot w}\dot w$) to the left. 4. Add $\dot\theta=q$. 5. Write $\mathbf E\dot{\mathbf x}=\mathbf A'\mathbf x$; if asked for $\mathbf A$, form $\mathbf E^{-1}\mathbf A'$.
> - **Common mistakes:** leaving $\mathring Z_{\dot w}\dot w$ on the right, so a derivative appears in the "state matrix"; forgetting the $mU_\infty q$ term from Eq. 5.7c when collecting the $q$ column.
> - **Practise:** [[#4. Assembling the longitudinal equations (5.17)]] · [[SESA3047 Chapter 5 Practice Problems - Solutions#Problem 3. The lateral state-space model|Practice problem 3]]

In 5.1 the left-hand sides of the force and moment equations became mass times perturbation acceleration. The right-hand sides, $X_1$, $Z_1$, $M_1$, are what push the aircraft once it is disturbed. Two things do that: gravity, whose body-axis components change when the nose pitches up or down, and the air, whose forces change with speed, angle of attack and pitch rate. Gravity can be linearised exactly from geometry. The aerodynamics cannot be derived in this module, so each change is assumed proportional to the perturbation that caused it, with a constant of proportionality called an aerodynamic derivative. Put both into Eqs. 5.7–5.8 and the result is four linear first-order ODEs.

Year 2 built the same model in [[SESA2027 A2 - Longitudinal State-Space Model and Aerodynamic Derivatives|SESA2027 A2]], including where the derivatives come from physically. This note follows the SESA3047 handout's notation and ordering.

## 1. The longitudinal–lateral split

Equations 5.7–5.8 fall into two groups that share no variables (handout p. 3):

- **longitudinal dynamics**: $u$, $w$, $q$ (with $\theta$), from $X_1$, $Z_1$, $M_1$;
- **lateral dynamics**: $v$, $p$, $r$ (with $\phi$), from $Y_1$, $L_1$, $N_1$.

![[amc_long_lat_split.png|820]]

The blocks are decoupled for three reasons, and all three need stating. The trim is symmetric (no sideslip, roll or turn rate). The aircraft is symmetric about its $x$–$z$ plane, so $J_{xy}=J_{yz}=0$ and pitch does not couple to roll or yaw through inertia. And, needed once aerodynamics is added, a symmetric aircraft's longitudinal forces do not change to first order with $v,p,r$, nor its lateral forces with $u,w,q$. Lose any of these, for example with a propeller's gyroscopic moment ([[SESA3047 5.1 - State-Space Form and Linearisation#4. Linearising the moment equations (5.8)|5.1 §4]]), and the $6\times6$ model no longer splits. The handout follows the longitudinal set; the lateral set is Practice problem 3.

## 2. Gravitational contribution (5.9–5.15)

L14 drew the aircraft climbing at angle $\gamma_0$ with $x$ along $U_\infty$ and $z$ perpendicular, below (ll. 373–390).

![[amc_gravity_perturbation.png|820]]

Resolving the weight $mg$ along these axes:

$$
X_g=-mg\sin\gamma,\qquad(5.9\text{a})
$$

$$
Z_g=mg\cos\gamma,\qquad(5.9\text{b})
$$

where $\gamma$ is the climb angle. These are general, not yet an equilibrium (ll. 401–410). At the trimmed condition:

$$
X_{g0}=-mg\sin\gamma_0,\qquad(5.10\text{a})
$$

$$
Z_{g0}=mg\cos\gamma_0.\qquad(5.10\text{b})
$$

Gravity and mass are constant (ll. 430–434), so the only perturbed quantities are the two forces and the angle (Eq. 5.11):

$$
X_g=X_{g0}+\epsilon X_1,\qquad Z_g=Z_{g0}+\epsilon Z_1,\qquad\theta=\gamma_0+\epsilon\theta_1.
$$

Here $X_1$, $Z_1$ are the gravity parts of the out-of-balance force (the handout reuses the symbols of Eq. 5.7; L14 writes $X_{g1}$, $Z_{g1}$, which is clearer). Substitute:

$$
X_{g0}+\epsilon X_1=-mg\sin(\gamma_0+\epsilon\theta_1),\qquad(5.12\text{a})
$$

$$
Z_{g0}+\epsilon Z_1=mg\cos(\gamma_0+\epsilon\theta_1).\qquad(5.12\text{b})
$$

### The X component

Expand with $\sin(A+B)=\sin A\cos B+\cos A\sin B$ (Eq. 5.13a):

$$
X_{g0}+\epsilon X_1=-mg\big(\sin\gamma_0\cos(\epsilon\theta_1)+\cos\gamma_0\sin(\epsilon\theta_1)\big).\qquad(5.14\text{a})
$$

Use $\cos(\epsilon\theta_1)\approx1$ and $\sin(\epsilon\theta_1)\approx\epsilon\theta_1$. Cosine is even, so its series starts at 1; sine is odd, so its series starts at the angle itself (ll. 449–460):

$$
X_{g0}+\epsilon X_1=-mg\sin\gamma_0-mg\cos\gamma_0\,\epsilon\theta_1.
$$

The $\epsilon^0$ terms are Eq. 5.10a, $X_{g0}=-mg\sin\gamma_0$, so they cancel. Getting the equilibrium back is the check that the expansion is right (ll. 469–478):

$$
\epsilon X_1=-mg\cos\gamma_0\,\epsilon\theta_1.
$$

Divide by $\epsilon$:

$$
\boxed{X_1=-mg\,\theta_1\cos\gamma_0.}\qquad(5.15\text{a})
$$

### The Z component

Expand with $\cos(A+B)=\cos A\cos B-\sin A\sin B$ (Eq. 5.13b):

$$
Z_{g0}+\epsilon Z_1=mg\big(\cos\gamma_0\cos(\epsilon\theta_1)-\sin\gamma_0\sin(\epsilon\theta_1)\big).\qquad(5.14\text{b})
$$

Apply the same small-angle forms:

$$
Z_{g0}+\epsilon Z_1=mg\cos\gamma_0-mg\sin\gamma_0\,\epsilon\theta_1.
$$

Cancel the trim terms (Eq. 5.10b) and divide by $\epsilon$:

$$
\boxed{Z_1=-mg\,\theta_1\sin\gamma_0.}\qquad(5.15\text{b})
$$

Both make physical sense. Pitch the nose up by $\theta_1$ in level flight ($\gamma_0=0$) and the weight acquires a backward component $-mg\theta_1$ along $x$, while the $z$ component changes only at second order. In a steep climb the $z$ component changes too. The trim parts never reach the final equations, because *"we're looking at departures to and from that equilibrium point"* (ll. 488–493).

> [!warning] $\theta$ or $\gamma$?
> L14 wrote the perturbation as $\gamma_1$ and said *"in the notes I use theta, theta is fine, gamma is fine, they're the same thing"* (l. 485). They agree only at trim. The body-axis components of gravity depend on the pitch angle $\theta$ ([[SESA3047 4.2 - Translational Dynamics|4.2]]: $X_g=-mg\sin\theta$). With $x$ along $U_\infty$ at trim, the trim angle of attack is zero, so $\theta_0=\gamma_0$. After a disturbance the axes rotate with the aircraft and the flight path does not follow exactly: $\gamma_1=\theta_1-\alpha_1$ with $\alpha_1\approx w_1/U_\infty$. The handout's Eq. 5.11 uses $\theta$ for the perturbed angle, which is right, and $\theta$ is the state in Eq. 5.18. Read L14's $\gamma_1$ as the pitch perturbation $\theta_1$.

## 3. Aerodynamic contribution (5.16)

*Written ahead from the handout; not yet lectured.*

The aerodynamic forces and pitching moment change when the aircraft is disturbed. Near trim, each change is taken as a linear combination of the perturbations:

$$
\Delta X_a=\mathring X_uu+\mathring X_ww,\qquad(5.16\text{a})
$$

$$
\Delta Z_a=\mathring Z_uu+\mathring Z_ww+\mathring Z_qq+\mathring Z_{\dot w}\dot w,\qquad(5.16\text{b})
$$

$$
\Delta M_a=\mathring M_uu+\mathring M_ww+\mathring M_qq+\mathring M_{\dot w}\dot w.\qquad(5.16\text{c})
$$

From here on the handout drops the subscript 1: $u$, $w$, $q$, $\theta$ are perturbations. Each coefficient is a partial derivative at trim, for example $\mathring Z_w=\partial Z/\partial w|_0$: a first-order Taylor expansion of the aerodynamic force in the perturbation variables. The ring marks the dimensional form (units such as N s/m); the dimensionless versions and the conversion are in §6.

The handout takes $\mathring X_q=\mathring X_{\dot w}=0$, so Eq. 5.16a has two terms. Strictly they are small rather than zero, and neglected by convention. The $\dot w$ derivatives are there because the tailplane feels the wing's downwash a moment late: a change in $w$ at the wing reaches the tail after the time the air takes to travel back, which looks to first order like a dependence on $\dot w$ (see [[Aerodynamic Stability Derivatives]] for the Year 2 estimates of each derivative).

Eq. 5.16 has no elevator or throttle terms. Without them Eq. 5.18 is the unforced model, $\dot{\mathbf x}=\mathbf A\mathbf x$, enough for stability; control terms belong to $\mathbf B$, which Dr Symon has said comes in Chapters 7–8 (L14 ll. 154–155).

## 4. Assembling the longitudinal equations (5.17)

*Written ahead from the handout; not yet lectured.*

Each perturbed equation is inertia side (Eqs. 5.7a, 5.7c, 5.8b) = gravity (5.15) + aerodynamics (5.16).

### Axial force

$$
m\dot u=X_{g1}+\Delta X_a.
$$

Substitute Eqs. 5.15a and 5.16a:

$$
\boxed{m\dot u=\mathring X_uu+\mathring X_ww-mg\cos\gamma_0\,\theta.}\qquad(5.17\text{a})
$$

### Normal force

$$
m(\dot w-qU_\infty)=Z_{g1}+\Delta Z_a.
$$

Substitute Eqs. 5.15b and 5.16b:

$$
m\dot w-mU_\infty q=-mg\sin\gamma_0\,\theta+\mathring Z_uu+\mathring Z_ww+\mathring Z_qq+\mathring Z_{\dot w}\dot w.
$$

Move the $\dot w$ term to the left and the $mU_\infty q$ term to the right:

$$
\boxed{(m-\mathring Z_{\dot w})\dot w=\mathring Z_uu+\mathring Z_ww+(\mathring Z_q+mU_\infty)q-mg\sin\gamma_0\,\theta.}\qquad(5.17\text{b})
$$

### Pitching moment

Gravity acts at the centre of mass and gives no moment, so only aerodynamics appears:

$$
J_{yy}\dot q=\Delta M_a.
$$

Substitute Eq. 5.16c and move the $\dot w$ term to the left:

$$
\boxed{-\mathring M_{\dot w}\dot w+J_{yy}\dot q=\mathring M_uu+\mathring M_ww+\mathring M_qq.}\qquad(5.17\text{c})
$$

### The fourth state

$\theta$ now appears on the right of Eqs. 5.17a–b, so it has to be a state and needs its own equation. The rotational kinematics of [[SESA3047 3.1 - Rotational Kinematics and Euler-Angle Rates|3.1]] give $\dot\theta=q\cos\phi-r\sin\phi$. Linearised about $\phi_0=0$, $q_0=r_0=0$, this is $\dot\theta_1=q_1$, the handout's $\dot\theta=q$.

## 5. The complete model and the state matrix (5.18)

Collect the four equations with $\mathbf x=[u,w,q,\theta]^T$:

$$
\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}m&0&0&0\\0&m-\mathring Z_{\dot w}&0&0\\0&-\mathring M_{\dot w}&J_{yy}&0\\0&0&0&1\end{bmatrix}}_{\mathbf E}
\begin{bmatrix}\dot u\\\dot w\\\dot q\\\dot\theta\end{bmatrix}
=
\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}\mathring X_u&\mathring X_w&0&-mg\cos\gamma_0\\\mathring Z_u&\mathring Z_w&\mathring Z_q+mU_\infty&-mg\sin\gamma_0\\\mathring M_u&\mathring M_w&\mathring M_q&0\\0&0&1&0\end{bmatrix}}_{\mathbf A'}
\begin{bmatrix}u\\w\\q\\\theta\end{bmatrix}.\qquad(5.18)
$$

The handout's key idea: everything on the left is a first-order time derivative of a state, everything on the right a linear combination of states. Eq. 5.18 is not yet in the form $\dot{\mathbf x}=\mathbf A\mathbf x$, because $\mathbf E$ is not the identity. ($\mathbf E$ and $\mathbf A'$ are labels used here, not the handout's.) $\mathbf E$ is lower triangular with non-zero diagonal as long as $m\neq\mathring Z_{\dot w}$, so it is invertible, and

$$
\mathbf A=\mathbf E^{-1}\mathbf A'.
$$

Invert row by row. Rows 1, 2 and 4 divide by $m$, $m-\mathring Z_{\dot w}$ and 1. Row 3 has $\dot q=(\mathring M_{\dot w}\dot w+\mathring M_uu+\mathring M_ww+\mathring M_qq)/J_{yy}$ with $\dot w$ from row 2 substituted. The result (checked with SymPy):

$$
\mathbf A=\begin{bmatrix}
\dfrac{\mathring X_u}{m}&\dfrac{\mathring X_w}{m}&0&-g\cos\gamma_0\\[8pt]
\dfrac{\mathring Z_u}{m-\mathring Z_{\dot w}}&\dfrac{\mathring Z_w}{m-\mathring Z_{\dot w}}&\dfrac{\mathring Z_q+mU_\infty}{m-\mathring Z_{\dot w}}&\dfrac{-mg\sin\gamma_0}{m-\mathring Z_{\dot w}}\\[8pt]
\dfrac{1}{J_{yy}}\Big(\mathring M_u+\dfrac{\mathring M_{\dot w}\mathring Z_u}{m-\mathring Z_{\dot w}}\Big)&\dfrac{1}{J_{yy}}\Big(\mathring M_w+\dfrac{\mathring M_{\dot w}\mathring Z_w}{m-\mathring Z_{\dot w}}\Big)&\dfrac{1}{J_{yy}}\Big(\mathring M_q+\dfrac{\mathring M_{\dot w}(\mathring Z_q+mU_\infty)}{m-\mathring Z_{\dot w}}\Big)&\dfrac{-\mathring M_{\dot w}mg\sin\gamma_0}{J_{yy}(m-\mathring Z_{\dot w})}\\[8pt]
0&0&1&0
\end{bmatrix}.
$$

Two checks on this. With $\mathring Z_{\dot w}=\mathring M_{\dot w}=0$ it reduces to dividing each row of $\mathbf A'$ by $m$, $m$, $J_{yy}$, 1. And in level flight ($\gamma_0=0$) the $\theta$ column is $[-g,0,0,0]^T$: a pitch perturbation in level flight only tilts the weight backwards along $x$.

The handout calls Eq. 5.18 *"NOT easy to obtain"* and repeats that the first step is to linearise about an operating point (p. 5). Its stability follows from the eigenvalues of $\mathbf A$ ([[SESA3047 5.4 - Time-Domain Solution and Stability|5.4]]); in Year 2 they gave the short-period and phugoid modes ([[SESA2027 A3 - Longitudinal Dynamic Modes - SPO and Phugoid]]).

## 6. Dimensional and dimensionless derivatives (appendix, 5.51–5.56)

*Written ahead from the handout; not yet lectured.*

A dimensional derivative is the dimensionless one times a reference force or moment and a reference for the variable it multiplies. Force derivatives scale with $\tfrac12\rho U_\infty^2S$, moment derivatives with $\tfrac12\rho U_\infty^2S\bar c$ (the handout writes $c$). The variable's reference is $U_\infty$ for $u$ and $w$, $U_\infty/c$ for $q$, and $U_\infty^2/c$ for $\dot w$:

| Dimensional | = dimensionless × reference × 1/(variable reference) | Simplified | Eq. |
|---|---|---|---|
| $\mathring Z_u$ | $Z_u\cdot\tfrac12\rho U_\infty^2S\cdot\dfrac{1}{U_\infty}$ | $Z_u\cdot\tfrac12\rho U_\infty S$ | 5.51 |
| $\mathring Z_q$ | $Z_q\cdot\tfrac12\rho U_\infty^2S\cdot\dfrac{c}{U_\infty}$ | $Z_q\cdot\tfrac12\rho U_\infty Sc$ | 5.52 |
| $\mathring Z_{\dot w}$ | $Z_{\dot w}\cdot\tfrac12\rho U_\infty^2S\cdot\dfrac{c}{U_\infty^2}$ | $Z_{\dot w}\cdot\tfrac12\rho Sc$ | 5.53 |
| $\mathring M_u$ | $M_u\cdot\tfrac12\rho U_\infty^2Sc\cdot\dfrac{1}{U_\infty}$ | $M_u\cdot\tfrac12\rho U_\infty Sc$ | 5.54 |
| $\mathring M_q$ | $M_q\cdot\tfrac12\rho U_\infty^2Sc\cdot\dfrac{c}{U_\infty}$ | $M_q\cdot\tfrac12\rho U_\infty Sc^2$ | 5.55 |
| $\mathring M_{\dot w}$ | $M_{\dot w}\cdot\tfrac12\rho U_\infty^2Sc\cdot\dfrac{c}{U_\infty^2}$ | $M_{\dot w}\cdot\tfrac12\rho Sc^2$ | 5.56 |

The handout lists only these six. The same pattern gives the others (not in the handout): $\mathring X_u=X_u\tfrac12\rho U_\infty S$, $\mathring X_w=X_w\tfrac12\rho U_\infty S$, $\mathring Z_w=Z_w\tfrac12\rho U_\infty S$ and $\mathring M_w=M_w\tfrac12\rho U_\infty Sc$.

A units check catches most slips. $\mathring Z_u$ multiplies a velocity and gives a force, so it must be in N s/m $=$ kg/s. $\tfrac12\rho U_\infty S$ has units $\text{kg m}^{-3}\cdot\text{m s}^{-1}\cdot\text{m}^2=\text{kg/s}$. $\mathring Z_{\dot w}$ multiplies an acceleration, so it must be in kg, and $\tfrac12\rho Sc$ is $\text{kg m}^{-3}\cdot\text{m}^3=\text{kg}$. That is why $m-\mathring Z_{\dot w}$ in Eq. 5.17b is dimensionally consistent: $\mathring Z_{\dot w}$ acts as added mass.
