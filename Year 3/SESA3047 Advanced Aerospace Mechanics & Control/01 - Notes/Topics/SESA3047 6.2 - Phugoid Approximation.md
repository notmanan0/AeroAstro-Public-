# SESA3047 6.2 - Phugoid Approximation

> [!abstract] Summary
> The phugoid is the slow, lightly damped longitudinal mode in which the aircraft trades speed for height at almost constant angle of attack. Holding the total energy and $C_L$ constant gives Lanchester's approximation, $\ddot h+(2g^2/U_\infty^2)h=0$, so $\omega_n=\sqrt2\,g/U_\infty$ and $\zeta=0$; for the F-4C that is 0.0779 rad/s against 0.0781 rad/s from the full model. Freezing the pitching moment ($\dot q=0$) at level trim and dropping $\mathring Z_q$, $\mathring Z_{\dot w}$, $\mathring M_q$ and $\mathring M_{\dot w}$ reduces the longitudinal model to three equations in $u$, $w$, $\theta$, whose characteristic equation (Eq. 6.38) gives the damping as well: $\omega_n=0.0797$ rad/s and $\zeta=0.095$ against 0.0781 and 0.083.

> [!summary]- Exam use: quick path
> - **Use it when:** asked to estimate the phugoid frequency (and damping) without solving the longitudinal quartic, to derive Lanchester's result, or to state and justify the assumptions behind a phugoid approximation.
> - **Assumptions:** Lanchester: total energy constant (thrust balances drag, nothing dissipated), $\alpha$ and $C_L$ fixed at trim, lift close to vertical (shallow flight path), $h$ measured from the trim height where $U=U_\infty$. Improved approximation: level trim $\gamma_0=0$, $\dot q=0$, $\mathring Z_q=\mathring Z_{\dot w}=\mathring M_q=\mathring M_{\dot w}=0$, small perturbations in FRD body axes, angles in radians.
> - **Method:** 1. Lanchester: $\omega_n=\sqrt2\,g/U_\infty$ ($U_\infty$ in m/s), $\zeta=0$, $T=2\pi/\omega_n$. 2. Improved: form $\mathring M_u\mathring Z_w/\mathring M_w-\mathring Z_u$ with dimensional derivatives, then $\omega_n^2=g/(mU_\infty)$ times it. 3. $2\zeta\omega_n=\frac1m\left[\frac{\mathring M_u}{\mathring M_w}\left(\mathring X_w-\frac{mg}{U_\infty}\right)-\mathring X_u\right]$. 4. Compare with the full model if $\mathbf A$ is given: $\omega_n=|\lambda|$, $\zeta=-\sigma/|\lambda|$. 5. Explain the physics: lift changing with speed at fixed $C_L$ sets the frequency, drag changing with speed sets the damping.
> - **Common mistakes:** writing $-mg$ in the (1,3) entry of Eq. 6.37 (moving $-mg\theta$ to the left makes it $+mg$; with $-mg$ the roots come out real and one is unstable); dropping the $\lambda$ from $-mU_\infty\lambda$, which multiplies $\dot\theta$.
> - **Practise:** [[#Worked example: Lanchester's approximation for the F-4C]] · [[#Worked example: the improved approximation for the F-4C]] · [[SESA2025 Past Paper 2013-14 - Solutions#Q3. Longitudinal modes, SPO approximation and Lanchester's phugoid (25 marks)|SESA2025 2013-14 Q3]]

The two longitudinal modes of [[SESA3047 6.1 - Longitudinal Modes, Eigenvectors and the SPO Approximation|6.1]] run on very different clocks. The F-4C's short-period oscillation has a period of 4.6 s and halves its amplitude in 1.9 s; its phugoid has a period of 81 s. By the time the phugoid has moved appreciably, the angle of attack and the pitch rate have long since settled to whatever the current speed demands, so the phugoid is carried by the speed $u$ and the pitch attitude $\theta$. The flight path rises and falls through tens of metres, the speed falls on the way up and rises on the way down, and the nose follows the flight path, $\theta\approx\gamma$, because $\alpha$ hardly changes.

Two approximations come out of that picture. Lanchester's uses energy alone: if nothing is dissipated, every metre of height costs a fixed amount of speed, and at fixed $C_L$ the lift, and with it the vertical force, follows the speed. That gives a simple harmonic oscillator for the height and, usually, a close estimate of the frequency, but no damping at all. The improved approximation starts from the linearised longitudinal equations instead and removes the terms that belong to the fast mode. It keeps the change of drag with speed, so it predicts the damping as well, at the cost of more algebra and six aerodynamic derivatives.

The phugoid approximations sit alongside the SPO approximation of [[SESA3047 6.1 - Longitudinal Modes, Eigenvectors and the SPO Approximation#7. The SPO approximation (6.22–6.27)|6.1 §7]]. Both rely on the same separation of time scales, applied the other way round: the SPO approximation freezes the slow states, and the phugoid approximation lets the fast ones settle.

## 1. The phugoid as an exchange of energy (Fig. 6.3)

An aircraft displaced above its trim height with the same total energy is slower there, and at fixed $C_L$ the lift $L=\tfrac12\rho U^2SC_L$ falls with the square of the speed, so lift is less than weight and the flight path curves downwards. On the way down the aircraft speeds up, and at the bottom of the path the lift exceeds the weight and curves the path upwards again. The restoring force on the height comes from the speed, and the speed comes from the height through the energy balance. That loop is the phugoid.

![[amc_ch6_phugoid_energy_exchange.png|900]]

The phases follow from the same loop. Speed is lowest at the crest and highest in the trough, so $u$ is in antiphase with $h$ (panel b). The flight-path angle is the slope of the path, $\gamma\approx\dot h/U_\infty$, which is largest halfway up the climb and zero at the crest: $\gamma$ leads $h$ by a quarter cycle. With $\alpha$ almost constant, $\theta$ moves with $\gamma$, so the nose is highest halfway up the climb and level at the top. The kinetic and potential energy swap back and forth while their sum stays fixed (panel c). Nothing in this picture involves the wing stalling: the angle of attack stays at its trim value, and lift falls below weight only because the speed falls.

The F-4C eigenvector of 6.1 confirms the picture. Normalised to $u=1$ m/s, its phugoid eigenvector has $w=0.140-0.007j$ m/s, a change in $\alpha$ of 0.14 times $u/U_\infty$, and $|\theta|=1.414\,|u|/U_\infty$ with $\theta$ lagging $u$ by $90.1^\circ$. The ratio $\sqrt2$ and the quarter-cycle lag are exactly what Lanchester's approximation predicts (§2).

## 2. Lanchester's approximation (6.28–6.33)

### Assumptions

- The total energy is constant: thrust balances drag throughout and nothing is dissipated.
- The angle of attack, and so $C_L$, stay at their trim values.
- The lift acts vertically, which needs a shallow flight path ($\cos\gamma\approx1$).
- The height $h$ is measured upwards from the trim height, where the speed is $U_\infty$; $\rho$ and $g$ are constant over the height change.

### Derivation

Constant total energy, with $U=U_\infty$ at $h=0$ (Eq. 6.28):

$$
\tfrac12mU_\infty^2=\tfrac12mU^2+mgh=\text{const.}\qquad(6.28)
$$

Divide by $\tfrac12m$ (Eq. 6.29):

$$
U_\infty^2=U^2+2gh.\qquad(6.29)
$$

At trim the lift balances the weight, which defines $C_L$ (Eq. 6.30):

$$
C_L=\frac{mg}{\tfrac12\rho U_\infty^2S}.\qquad(6.30)
$$

Multiply Eq. 6.29 by $\tfrac12\rho SC_L$:

$$
\tfrac12\rho U_\infty^2SC_L=\tfrac12\rho U^2SC_L+\rho SC_Lgh .
$$

Move the height term across (Eq. 6.31):

$$
\tfrac12\rho U^2SC_L=\tfrac12\rho U_\infty^2SC_L-\rho SC_Lgh.\qquad(6.31)
$$

The left-hand side is the lift $L$ at the current speed. By Eq. 6.30 the first term on the right is $mg$:

$$
L=mg-\rho SC_Lgh .
$$

Newton's second law vertically, with $h$ up and the lift vertical (Eq. 6.32):

$$
m\ddot h=L-mg.\qquad(6.32)
$$

Substitute the lift:

$$
m\ddot h=-\rho SC_Lgh .
$$

Substitute $C_L$ from Eq. 6.30:

$$
m\ddot h=-\rho Sg\,\frac{mg}{\tfrac12\rho U_\infty^2S}\,h .
$$

Cancel $\rho$ and $S$:

$$
m\ddot h=-\frac{mg^2}{\tfrac12U_\infty^2}\,h .
$$

Divide by $m$ and clear the half:

$$
\ddot h=-\frac{2g^2}{U_\infty^2}\,h .
$$

Collect on one side (Eq. 6.33):

$$
\ddot h+\frac{2g^2}{U_\infty^2}\,h=0.\qquad(6.33)
$$

Substitute the ansatz $h=He^{\lambda t}$, so $\ddot h=\lambda^2He^{\lambda t}$, and divide by $e^{\lambda t}$ (the Laplace transform gives the same with $s$ for $\lambda$):

$$
\left(\lambda^2+\frac{2g^2}{U_\infty^2}\right)H=0 .
$$

Compare with the standard form $\lambda^2+2\zeta\omega_n\lambda+\omega_n^2=0$, which is on the exam formula sheet (L18 l. 352). There is no $\lambda$ term, so

$$
\zeta=0,\qquad\omega_n^2=\frac{2g^2}{U_\infty^2},\qquad\boxed{\omega_n=\frac{\sqrt2\,g}{U_\infty}},\qquad T=\frac{2\pi}{\omega_n}=\frac{\sqrt2\,\pi U_\infty}{g}.
$$

The roots are $\lambda=\pm j\omega_n$, on the imaginary axis. Within this approximation the period is proportional to the trim speed and independent of the aircraft's mass, wing area and aerodynamic derivatives; §4 shows where those come back in.

Equation 6.33 is linear in $h$ without any small-perturbation step: at fixed $C_L$, $L\propto U^2=U_\infty^2-2gh$ exactly. The only approximations are the physical ones in the list above, chiefly constant energy and a vertical lift.

### Why zero damping is a fair first answer

A real phugoid is lightly damped: its eigenvalues lie close to the imaginary axis, so it decays, or grows, over many cycles. The F-4C's has $\zeta=0.083$ and the Eq. 6.4 example's $\zeta=0.018$. A model with $\zeta=0$ is therefore close to the truth for the frequency and the shape of the motion. What it cannot do is say whether the phugoid is stable, because the small damping it leaves out decides that. The damping comes from the drag changing with speed, which the assumption of constant energy removes by letting thrust balance drag at every instant.

### Speed, height and pitch in the Lanchester phugoid

Write $U=U_\infty+u$ in Eq. 6.29:

$$
U_\infty^2=U_\infty^2+2U_\infty u+u^2+2gh .
$$

Drop $u^2$ for a small speed change and cancel $U_\infty^2$:

$$
u=-\frac{g}{U_\infty}\,h .
$$

Speed is in antiphase with height. With $h=h_0\cos\omega_nt$, the speed amplitude is $u_0=gh_0/U_\infty$, so a speed change $u_0$ goes with a height change

$$
h_0=\frac{U_\infty u_0}{g}.
$$

The flight-path angle for a shallow path is $\gamma\approx\dot h/U_\infty$:

$$
\gamma=-\frac{\omega_nh_0}{U_\infty}\sin\omega_nt .
$$

The ratio of the amplitudes of $\gamma$ and $u/U_\infty$ is

$$
\frac{|\gamma|}{|u|/U_\infty}=\frac{\omega_nh_0/U_\infty}{gh_0/U_\infty^2}=\frac{\omega_nU_\infty}{g}=\sqrt2 .
$$

At constant $\alpha$ the perturbation in $\theta$ equals the perturbation in $\gamma$, so $|\theta|=\sqrt2\,|u|/U_\infty$, with $\theta$ a quarter cycle behind $u$ and a quarter cycle ahead of $h$.

### Worked example: Lanchester's approximation for the F-4C

The F-4C of Table 6.1 flies level at $U_\infty=178$ m/s. Then

$$
\omega_n=\frac{\sqrt2\times9.81}{178}=\frac{13.873}{178}=0.0779\ \text{rad/s},\qquad T=\frac{2\pi}{0.07794}=80.6\ \text{s}.
$$

The full model of Eq. 6.22 gives $\lambda=-0.00645\pm0.07788j$, so $\omega_n=0.0781$ rad/s, $\zeta=0.0825$ and $T=80.7$ s. Lanchester's frequency is 0.26 % low, and its period is within 0.1 s.

For a speed disturbance of $u_0=5$ m/s, the height swings by $h_0=178\times5/9.81=90.7$ m either side of trim, and the pitch attitude by $\sqrt2\times5/178=0.0397$ rad $=2.28^\circ$. At the crest the speed is 173 m/s and $L/mg=(173/178)^2=0.945$; in the trough it is 183 m/s and $L/mg=1.057$ (panel a of the figure in §1).

> [!check] Trim lift coefficient
> Eq. 6.30 with $\rho=0.3809$ kg/m³ and $S=49.239$ m² gives $\tfrac12\rho U_\infty^2S=297\,119$ N and $C_L=173\,068/297\,119=0.582$. It drops out of $\omega_n$, as the derivation shows it must.

## 3. Which states matter: time scales and coupling

Each longitudinal mode is dominated by two of the four states. In the eigenvectors of [[SESA3047 6.1 - Longitudinal Modes, Eigenvectors and the SPO Approximation#4. Eigenvectors and modes (6.17–6.18)|6.1 §4]], the largest component of the phugoid is $u$ and the largest of the SPO is $w$ (in the raw units of the state vector). The SPO is mainly a coupling between $w$ and $q$, the phugoid mainly a coupling between $u$ and $\theta$ (L18 ll. 411–420).

Coupling here means off-diagonal terms. If a state matrix were diagonal, each equation would read $\dot x_i=a_{ii}x_i$, every state would evolve on its own, and each eigenvector would be a single state. Any off-diagonal entry makes the rate of one state depend on another. The longitudinal $\mathbf A$ is nearly full, so all four states are coupled, and a disturbance to any one of them excites the others ([[State Coupling]]). Coupling between states runs through the whole module, from the geometric and inertial coupling of Lab 1 Exercise 3 to the modes here, and it is one of the module's central ideas (L18 ll. 421–436).

The approximations keep the coupling that matters for one mode and remove the rest. That works because the modes are well separated in time ([[Separation of Time Scales]]). For the F-4C the phugoid period is 17 times the SPO period, and the SPO halves in 1.9 s, so within a few seconds of any disturbance the fast states have adjusted to the slow ones. Over a phugoid cycle the pitching moment is then always in balance, which is the statement $\dot q\approx0$, and the pitch rate and the rate of change of $w$ are both small. The terms they multiply, $\mathring Z_q q$, $\mathring M_q q$, $\mathring Z_{\dot w}\dot w$ and $\mathring M_{\dot w}\dot w$, are dropped.

> [!check] How small the dropped terms are
> Along the F-4C's full phugoid eigenvector (normalised to $u=1$ m/s), the pitching-moment row of Eq. 6.1 has $|\mathring M_uu|=277.5$, $|\mathring M_ww|=247.7$ and, among the dropped terms, $|\mathring M_qq|=31.5$ (11 % of $\mathring M_uu$), $|J_{yy}\dot q|=8.0$ (2.9 %) and $|\mathring M_{\dot w}\dot w|=1.4$ (0.5 %). In the $Z$ row $\mathring Z_q$ is 0.3 % of $mU_\infty$ and $\mathring Z_{\dot w}$ is 0.1 % of $m$. So $\mathring M_q$ is the largest term left out: putting $\mathring M_q\dot\theta$ back into the moment balance moves the estimate from $\omega_n=0.0797$, $\zeta=0.095$ to $0.0779$, $0.089$, against the full model's $0.0781$, $0.083$.

## 4. The improved approximation (6.34–6.38)

### Assumptions

- Level trim, $\gamma_0=0$, so $\cos\gamma_0=1$ and $\sin\gamma_0=0$.
- The pitch rate varies slowly: $\dot q=0$, so the pitching moment stays in balance.
- $\mathring Z_q=\mathring Z_{\dot w}=0$, small beside $mU_\infty$ and $m$.
- $\mathring M_q=\mathring M_{\dot w}=0$, because $q$ and $\dot w$ are small in the phugoid.
- Small perturbations about trim, FRD body axes ($w$ positive down), $\theta$ in radians.

### Reducing the equations (6.34–6.36)

The longitudinal model of [[SESA3047 6.1 - Longitudinal Modes, Eigenvectors and the SPO Approximation#1. The longitudinal model as a state equation (6.1–6.3)|6.1 §1]], with states $[u,w,q,\theta]$, is

$$
\begin{bmatrix}m&0&0&0\\0&m-\mathring Z_{\dot w}&0&0\\0&-\mathring M_{\dot w}&J_{yy}&0\\0&0&0&1\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}\dot u\\\dot w\\\dot q\\\dot\theta\end{bmatrix}
=\begin{bmatrix}\mathring X_u&\mathring X_w&0&-mg\cos\gamma_0\\\mathring Z_u&\mathring Z_w&\mathring Z_q+mU_\infty&-mg\sin\gamma_0\\\mathring M_u&\mathring M_w&\mathring M_q&0\\0&0&1&0\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}u\\w\\q\\\theta\end{bmatrix}.\qquad(6.1)
$$

Apply the assumptions: $\gamma_0=0$, the four derivatives set to zero, and $\dot q=0$ in the vector on the left (Eq. 6.34):

$$
\begin{bmatrix}m&0&0&0\\0&m&0&0\\0&0&J_{yy}&0\\0&0&0&1\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}\dot u\\\dot w\\0\\\dot\theta\end{bmatrix}
=\begin{bmatrix}\mathring X_u&\mathring X_w&0&-mg\\\mathring Z_u&\mathring Z_w&mU_\infty&0\\\mathring M_u&\mathring M_w&0&0\\0&0&1&0\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}u\\w\\q\\\theta\end{bmatrix}.\qquad(6.34)
$$

![[amc_ch6_phugoid_reduction.png|900]]

Three moves turn this into three equations in three unknowns.

First, multiply out the third row of the left-hand side: $0\cdot\dot u+0\cdot\dot w+J_{yy}\cdot0+0\cdot\dot\theta=0$. $J_{yy}$ multiplies zero and drops out, and the pitching-moment equation keeps no derivative at all. It is not removed, though: it becomes the algebraic condition

$$
0=\mathring M_uu+\mathring M_ww,
$$

which says that the speed-dependent moment and the angle-of-attack-dependent moment cancel at every instant. For the F-4C it ties $w$ to $u$: $w=-(\mathring M_u/\mathring M_w)u=0.157\,u$.

Second, the fourth row only says $\dot\theta=q$. Use it to replace $q$ by $\dot\theta$ in the vector on the right; the only non-zero entry of the $q$ column is $mU_\infty$ in row 2. After that substitution the fourth row adds nothing, so it goes, together with the $\dot\theta$ entry on the left and the 1 in the $q$ column of its own row: that 1 existed only to relate $\theta$ and $q$ (L18 ll. 619–632). What remains is Eq. 6.35:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}m&0&0\\0&m&0\\0&0&0\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}\dot u\\\dot w\\0\end{bmatrix}
=\begin{bmatrix}\mathring X_u&\mathring X_w&0&-mg\\\mathring Z_u&\mathring Z_w&mU_\infty&0\\\mathring M_u&\mathring M_w&0&0\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}u\\w\\\dot\theta\\\theta\end{bmatrix}.\qquad(6.35)
$$

Third, Eq. 6.35 has a derivative, $\dot\theta$, on the right-hand side, so it is not in the form $\mathbf M\dot{\mathbf x}=\mathbf A'\mathbf x$. Row 2 reads

$$
m\dot w=\mathring Z_uu+\mathring Z_ww+mU_\infty\dot\theta .
$$

Subtract $mU_\infty\dot\theta$ from both sides:

$$
m\dot w-mU_\infty\dot\theta=\mathring Z_uu+\mathring Z_ww .
$$

The $\dot\theta$ term now sits on the left with coefficient $-mU_\infty$, and the $\dot\theta$ column on the right is empty. With $\mathbf x=[u,w,\theta]^T$ (Eq. 6.36):

$$
\begin{bmatrix}m&0&0\\0&m&-mU_\infty\\0&0&0\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}\dot u\\\dot w\\\dot\theta\end{bmatrix}
=\begin{bmatrix}\mathring X_u&\mathring X_w&-mg\\\mathring Z_u&\mathring Z_w&0\\\mathring M_u&\mathring M_w&0\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}u\\w\\\theta\end{bmatrix}.\qquad(6.36)
$$

Multiplying out both sides recovers the three equations, so nothing has changed except the bookkeeping. The new $\mathbf M$ has a row of zeros, so it is singular and $\mathbf A=\mathbf M^{-1}\mathbf A'$ cannot be formed. The eigenvalues come straight from $\det(\lambda\mathbf M-\mathbf A')=0$ instead. Because $\mathbf M$ has rank 2, that determinant is a quadratic in $\lambda$: three equations, but only two eigenvalues, the phugoid pair.

### The eigenvalue problem (6.37)

Use the ansatz that every state varies exponentially with the same exponent,

$$
u=u_0e^{\lambda t},\qquad w=w_0e^{\lambda t},\qquad\theta=\theta_0e^{\lambda t},
$$

so each time derivative is the same function multiplied by $\lambda$, for example $\dot u=\lambda u_0e^{\lambda t}$. Here $u_0$, $w_0$, $\theta_0$ are constant coefficients, the components of the eigenvector. Substitute into Eq. 6.36:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}m\lambda&0&0\\0&m\lambda&-mU_\infty\lambda\\0&0&0\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}u_0\\w_0\\\theta_0\end{bmatrix}e^{\lambda t}
=\begin{bmatrix}\mathring X_u&\mathring X_w&-mg\\\mathring Z_u&\mathring Z_w&0\\\mathring M_u&\mathring M_w&0\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}u_0\\w_0\\\theta_0\end{bmatrix}e^{\lambda t}.
$$

Divide by $e^{\lambda t}$, which is never zero, and move the right-hand side to the left (Eq. 6.37):

$$
\begin{bmatrix}m\lambda-\mathring X_u&-\mathring X_w&mg\\-\mathring Z_u&m\lambda-\mathring Z_w&-mU_\infty\lambda\\-\mathring M_u&-\mathring M_w&0\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}u_0\\w_0\\\theta_0\end{bmatrix}=\mathbf 0.\qquad(6.37)
$$

The (1,3) entry is $+mg$ because $-mg\theta$ moved to the left. The (2,3) entry carries $\lambda$ because it multiplies $\dot\theta$, not $\theta$. A non-zero eigenvector needs the determinant of the matrix to vanish.

### The characteristic equation by cofactors (6.38)

The cofactor expansion and Eq. 6.38 are handout only (L18 ll. 657–658). Expand along row 3, whose last entry is zero. The cofactor signs for positions (3,1) and (3,2) are $+$ and $-$:

$$
\det=(-\mathring M_u)\begin{vmatrix}-\mathring X_w&mg\\m\lambda-\mathring Z_w&-mU_\infty\lambda\end{vmatrix}-(-\mathring M_w)\begin{vmatrix}m\lambda-\mathring X_u&mg\\-\mathring Z_u&-mU_\infty\lambda\end{vmatrix}.
$$

The first $2\times2$ determinant is

$$
(-\mathring X_w)(-mU_\infty\lambda)-mg\left(m\lambda-\mathring Z_w\right)=mU_\infty\mathring X_w\lambda-m^2g\lambda+mg\mathring Z_w .
$$

The second is

$$
\left(m\lambda-\mathring X_u\right)(-mU_\infty\lambda)-mg(-\mathring Z_u)=-m^2U_\infty\lambda^2+mU_\infty\mathring X_u\lambda+mg\mathring Z_u .
$$

Substitute both:

$$
\det=-\mathring M_u\left(mU_\infty\mathring X_w\lambda-m^2g\lambda+mg\mathring Z_w\right)+\mathring M_w\left(-m^2U_\infty\lambda^2+mU_\infty\mathring X_u\lambda+mg\mathring Z_u\right).
$$

Group by powers of $\lambda$:

$$
\det=-m^2U_\infty\mathring M_w\lambda^2+m\left(mg\mathring M_u-U_\infty\mathring M_u\mathring X_w+U_\infty\mathring M_w\mathring X_u\right)\lambda-mg\left(\mathring M_u\mathring Z_w-\mathring M_w\mathring Z_u\right).
$$

Set it to zero and divide by $-m^2U_\infty\mathring M_w$, which is non-zero for a statically stable aircraft ($\mathring M_w<0$):

$$
\lambda^2+\frac{U_\infty\mathring M_u\mathring X_w-mg\mathring M_u-U_\infty\mathring M_w\mathring X_u}{mU_\infty\mathring M_w}\,\lambda+\frac{g\left(\mathring M_u\mathring Z_w-\mathring M_w\mathring Z_u\right)}{mU_\infty\mathring M_w}=0 .
$$

Split the constant term:

$$
\frac{g\left(\mathring M_u\mathring Z_w-\mathring M_w\mathring Z_u\right)}{mU_\infty\mathring M_w}=\frac{g}{mU_\infty}\left(\frac{\mathring M_u\mathring Z_w}{\mathring M_w}-\mathring Z_u\right).
$$

Split the $\lambda$ coefficient term by term:

$$
\frac{U_\infty\mathring M_u\mathring X_w-mg\mathring M_u-U_\infty\mathring M_w\mathring X_u}{mU_\infty\mathring M_w}=\frac1m\left[\frac{\mathring M_u}{\mathring M_w}\left(\mathring X_w-\frac{mg}{U_\infty}\right)-\mathring X_u\right].
$$

Matching with $\lambda^2+2\zeta\omega_n\lambda+\omega_n^2=0$ gives Eq. 6.38:

$$
\boxed{\omega_n^2=\frac{g}{mU_\infty}\left(\frac{\mathring M_u}{\mathring M_w}\mathring Z_w-\mathring Z_u\right),\qquad
\zeta=\frac{1}{2\omega_n}\,\frac1m\left[\frac{\mathring M_u}{\mathring M_w}\left(\mathring X_w-\frac{mg}{U_\infty}\right)-\mathring X_u\right].}\qquad(6.38)
$$

The derivatives are the dimensional ones of Eq. 6.22, and the result holds at level trim only.

### Worked example: the improved approximation for the F-4C

The six derivatives needed, from Table 6.1 converted as in [[SESA3047 6.1 - Longitudinal Modes, Eigenvectors and the SPO Approximation#Dimensional derivatives and the full model|6.1 §7]], with $m=17642$ kg, $U_\infty=178$ m/s and $g=9.81$ m/s²:

| Derivative | Value |
|---|---|
| $\mathring X_u$ | $-126.86$ kg/s |
| $\mathring X_w$ | $80.62$ kg/s |
| $\mathring Z_u$ | $-1214.0$ kg/s |
| $\mathring Z_w$ | $-5215.4$ kg/s |
| $\mathring M_u$ | $277.47$ kg m/s |
| $\mathring M_w$ | $-1770.1$ kg m/s |

The ratio that appears twice is $\mathring M_u/\mathring M_w=277.47/(-1770.1)=-0.15675$. The bracket in $\omega_n^2$ is

$$
\frac{\mathring M_u}{\mathring M_w}\mathring Z_w-\mathring Z_u=(-0.15675)(-5215.4)+1214.0=817.5+1214.0=2031.6\ \text{kg/s},
$$

so

$$
\omega_n^2=\frac{9.81}{17642\times178}\times2031.6=\frac{19\,930}{3\,140\,276}=6.346\times10^{-3}\ \text{s}^{-2},\qquad\omega_n=0.0797\ \text{rad/s}.
$$

The $\lambda$ coefficient has three terms:

$$
\frac{\mathring M_u}{\mathring M_w}\frac{\mathring X_w}{m}=(-0.15675)(0.004570)=-0.000716\ \text{s}^{-1},
$$

$$
-\frac{\mathring M_u}{\mathring M_w}\frac{g}{U_\infty}=(0.15675)(0.055112)=0.008639\ \text{s}^{-1},
$$

$$
-\frac{\mathring X_u}{m}=\frac{126.86}{17642}=0.007191\ \text{s}^{-1}.
$$

Their sum is $2\zeta\omega_n=0.015114$ s⁻¹, so

$$
\zeta=\frac{0.015114}{2\times0.07966}=0.0949 .
$$

The roots are $\lambda=-\zeta\omega_n\pm j\omega_n\sqrt{1-\zeta^2}=-0.00756\pm0.0793j$, with $T=79.2$ s and $t_{1/2}=\ln0.5/(-0.00756)=91.7$ s. The full model gives $\omega_n=0.0781$, $\zeta=0.0825$, $T=80.7$ s and $t_{1/2}=107.5$ s. The approximation overestimates the frequency by 1.9 % and the damping by 15 %, but unlike Lanchester's it predicts a stable, lightly damped phugoid of about the right decay rate.

The drag term $-\mathring X_u/m$ supplies 48 % of $2\zeta\omega_n$. The term $-(\mathring M_u/\mathring M_w)(g/U_\infty)$ supplies 57 %: it comes from the gravity entry $mg$ of the $X$ equation acting together with the moment balance, which makes $w$, and so the angle of attack, follow the speed. The small $\mathring X_w$ term takes back 5 %.

> [!check] Two independent routes to the same roots
> Solving $\det(\lambda\mathbf M-\mathbf A')=0$ numerically for the $3\times3$ matrices of Eq. 6.36 gives two finite eigenvalues, $-0.00756\pm0.0793j$, the same as Eq. 6.38. So does eliminating $w=0.157\,u$ with the moment balance and writing the remaining two equations as a $2\times2$ state matrix in $[u,\theta]$. Eq. 6.38 was also confirmed symbolically.

### Lanchester as a special case of Eq. 6.38

Lanchester's result sits inside Eq. 6.38. At fixed $C_L$ and small $\alpha$, the aerodynamic $Z$ force (FRD, $z$ down) is minus the lift:

$$
Z\approx-\tfrac12\rho U^2SC_L .
$$

Differentiate with respect to the speed at trim:

$$
\mathring Z_u=-\rho U_\infty SC_L .
$$

Substitute the trim $C_L$ of Eq. 6.30:

$$
\mathring Z_u=-\rho U_\infty S\,\frac{mg}{\tfrac12\rho U_\infty^2S}=-\frac{2mg}{U_\infty}.
$$

With no speed dependence of the pitching moment, $\mathring M_u=0$, Eq. 6.38 then gives

$$
\omega_n^2=\frac{g}{mU_\infty}\cdot\frac{2mg}{U_\infty}=\frac{2g^2}{U_\infty^2},
$$

which is Eq. 6.33, while the damping reduces to $2\zeta\omega_n=-\mathring X_u/m$: the drag term that Lanchester's energy argument leaves out. For the F-4C, $2mg/U_\infty=1944.6$ kg/s, but the real $-\mathring Z_u$ is only 1214.0 kg/s; the speed-stability term $\mathring M_u\mathring Z_w/\mathring M_w=817.5$ kg/s makes up the difference and slightly overshoots it (2031.6). Lanchester's near-perfect frequency for this aircraft is partly two effects cancelling.

## 6. The feedback loop of Lab 2

The control picture behind Lab 2 is the feedback loop of [[Open-Loop, Feedback and Feedforward Control]]. The plant $G_p$ describes what the system does by itself, with no control. A reference $r$ is what the output should do; the output $y$ is what it actually does. In open-loop control $r$ drives the plant directly. That is used in some mechanisms but seldom for the rigid-body dynamics of aircraft and spacecraft, which are normally under feedback control. Closing the loop feeds $y$ back to a summing junction that forms the error $e=r-y$. The controller turns the error into an actuator command $u$, the input to the plant; in Task 3 of Lab 2, the Boeing 747 altitude hold, $u$ is the elevator deflection. The control input $u$ is a different quantity from the speed perturbation $u$ of §§1–4; the course uses the same letter for both.

Task 1 of the lab is the plant on its own: for a single-input, single-output system the state-space and transfer-function blocks are equivalent descriptions and give the same response. Task 2 closes the loop with three controllers (PD control, a low-gain PD and PD with a notch filter). Why the three behave differently is the open question the lab report is expected to discuss, through what each controller does to the closed-loop poles (L18 ll. 160–226; [[SESA3047 6.1 - Longitudinal Modes, Eigenvectors and the SPO Approximation#7a. Simulink: feedback around a transfer function|6.1 §7a]]). What Lab 1 showed about coupling between states is the other theme to draw out (ll. 423–425). The report rules and the Mon 30 Nov deadline are in [[SESA3047 Advanced Aerospace Mechanics & Control Hub#Labs and coursework|the hub]].
