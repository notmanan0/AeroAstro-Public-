# SESA3043 1.3 - Potential-Flow Review

> [!abstract] Summary
> Potential flow is the irrotational limit of incompressible flow. Writing $\mathbf u=\nabla\phi$ makes continuity become Laplace's equation and makes momentum integrable to Bernoulli. In two dimensions a streamfunction $\psi$ satisfies continuity automatically; irrotationality then makes $\psi$ harmonic as well. Curves of constant $\psi$ are streamlines, the difference in $\psi$ between two streamlines is volume flow per unit span, and $\phi$- and $\psi$-contours intersect orthogonally. The Week 1 lecture ends after reviewing the elementary uniform, source/sink, vortex and doublet solutions. Complex potential begins next week.

## 1. From Navier–Stokes to potential flow

Start with incompressible Navier–Stokes and neglect body force for the lecture derivation:

$$
\nabla\cdot\mathbf u=0,
$$

$$
\frac{\partial\mathbf u}{\partial t}
+(\mathbf u\cdot\nabla)\mathbf u
=-\frac1\rho\nabla p+\nu\nabla^2\mathbf u.
$$

Potential flow assumes

$$
\boldsymbol\omega=\nabla\times\mathbf u=0.
$$

On a simply connected domain this implies the existence of a scalar velocity potential:

$$
\boxed{\mathbf u=\nabla\phi},
\qquad
u_i=\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x_i}.
$$

### Continuity becomes Laplace

$$
\nabla\cdot\mathbf u
=\nabla\cdot(\nabla\phi)
=\boxed{\nabla^2\phi=0}.
$$

Thus $\phi$ is harmonic.

### Momentum becomes Bernoulli

Use

$$
(\mathbf u\cdot\nabla)\mathbf u
=\nabla\left(\frac12|\mathbf u|^2\right)
-\mathbf u\times(\nabla\times\mathbf u).
$$

The vorticity term vanishes. Also $\nabla^2\mathbf u=\nabla(\nabla^2\phi)=0$. Therefore

$$
\nabla\left[
\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t}
+\frac12|\nabla\phi|^2
+\frac p\rho
\right]=0.
$$

Integrate in space:

$$
\boxed{
\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t}
+\frac12|\mathbf u|^2
+\frac p\rho=C(t)
}.
$$

For steady flow this reduces to the familiar Bernoulli constant. If gravity is retained, add $gz$.

## 2. Streamfunction for two-dimensional incompressible flow

Define

$$
\boxed{
u=\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial y},
\qquad
v=-\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial x}
}.
$$

Then

$$
\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}+\frac{\partial v}{\partial y}
=\frac{\partial^2\psi}{\partial x\partial y}
-\frac{\partial^2\psi}{\partial y\partial x}=0,
$$

so continuity is satisfied identically.

For irrotational flow,

$$
0=\omega_z=\frac{\partial v}{\partial x}-\frac{\partial u}{\partial y}
=-\left(
\frac{\partial^2\psi}{\partial x^2}
+\frac{\partial^2\psi}{\partial y^2}
\right).
$$

Hence

$$
\boxed{\nabla^2\psi=0}.
$$

## 3. Why constant ψ is a streamline

Along a curve $\psi(x,y)=\text{constant}$,

$$
\mathrm d\psi=\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial x}\mathrm dx
+\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial y}\mathrm dy=0.
$$

Using $\partial\psi/\partial x=-v$ and $\partial\psi/\partial y=u$,

$$
-v\,\mathrm dx+u\,\mathrm dy=0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\boxed{\frac{\mathrm dy}{\mathrm dx}=\frac vu}.
$$

This is exactly the tangent condition for a streamline. No flow crosses a streamline, so an impermeable solid boundary can be represented by $\psi=\text{constant}$.

## 4. Flow-rate meaning of ψ

Between two nearby streamlines, the differential volume flow per unit span is

$$
\mathrm dq=u\,\mathrm dy-v\,\mathrm dx.
$$

But

$$
\mathrm d\psi
=\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial x}\mathrm dx
+\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial y}\mathrm dy
=-v\,\mathrm dx+u\,\mathrm dy.
$$

Therefore $\mathrm dq=\mathrm d\psi$, and integration gives

$$
\boxed{q'=\psi_2-\psi_1},
$$

where $q'$ is volume flow rate per unit span between the two streamlines.

## 5. Orthogonality of $\phi$ and $\psi$

In Cartesian coordinates,

$$
u=\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x}=\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial y},
\qquad
v=\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial y}=-\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial x}.
$$

Then

$$
\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla\psi
=u(-v)+vu=0.
$$

Contours of velocity potential and streamfunction meet at right angles wherever the velocity is non-zero.

## 6. Cartesian and polar formulae

| Representation | Cartesian | Polar |
|---|---|---|
| Velocity potential | $u=\phi_x,\ v=\phi_y$ | $u_r=\phi_r,\ u_\theta=\frac1r\phi_\theta$ |
| Streamfunction | $u=\psi_y,\ v=-\psi_x$ | $u_r=\frac1r\psi_\theta,\ u_\theta=-\psi_r$ |

## 7. Elementary solutions and superposition

Because Laplace's equation is linear, any sum of harmonic potentials is also a solution. The four building blocks are reviewed in [[Elementary Potential Flows]]:

| Flow | Velocity potential $\phi$ | Streamfunction $\psi$ |
|---|---|---|
| Uniform flow in $+x$ | $U_\infty r\cos\theta$ | $U_\infty r\sin\theta$ |
| Source/sink of strength $\Lambda$ (slides: $m$) | $\dfrac{\Lambda}{2\pi}\ln r$ | $\dfrac{\Lambda}{2\pi}\theta$ |
| Point vortex, counter-clockwise $\Gamma>0$ | $\dfrac{\Gamma}{2\pi}\theta$ | $-\dfrac{\Gamma}{2\pi}\ln r$ |
| Doublet of strength $\kappa$ (slides: $\mu$), source upstream | $\dfrac{\kappa}{2\pi r}\cos\theta$ | $-\dfrac{\kappa}{2\pi r}\sin\theta$ |

Superposition builds practical flows such as the circular-cylinder solution.

> [!warning] Vortex sign on the SESA3043 slide
> The slide table (CH1-3, slide 21) gives $\phi=\dfrac{\Gamma}{2\pi}\theta$ and $u_\theta=\dfrac{\Gamma}{2\pi r}$, which make **counter-clockwise** circulation positive. It also gives $\psi=+\dfrac{\Gamma}{2\pi}\ln r$, but $u_\theta=-\partial\psi/\partial r$ then gives $u_\theta=-\Gamma/2\pi r$, which is **clockwise**. The slide therefore mixes the two conventions. The row above uses the counter-clockwise set, matching $\phi$ and the slide's velocity column. Confirm with the lecturer before an exam. The two self-consistent sets are:
>
> | Convention | $\phi$ | $\psi$ | $u_\theta$ |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | Counter-clockwise $\Gamma>0$ | $\dfrac{\Gamma}{2\pi}\theta$ | $-\dfrac{\Gamma}{2\pi}\ln r$ | $\dfrac{\Gamma}{2\pi r}$ |
> | Clockwise $\Gamma>0$ ([[SESA2022 T3 - Potential Flow\|SESA2022]]) | $-\dfrac{\Gamma}{2\pi}\theta$ | $\dfrac{\Gamma}{2\pi}\ln r$ | $-\dfrac{\Gamma}{2\pi r}$ |

> [!tip] Translating from SESA2022
> SESA2022 worked in the $x$–$z$ plane: $u=\partial\psi/\partial z$, $w=-\partial\psi/\partial x$, with $y$ into the page. SESA3043 uses the $x$–$y$ plane: $u=\partial\psi/\partial y$, $v=-\partial\psi/\partial x$. Substitute $z\to y$ and $w\to v$ and the algebra is identical. The difference is the vorticity component: SESA2022's $\omega_y=\partial u/\partial z-\partial w/\partial x$ is the **negative** of SESA3043's $\omega_z=\partial v/\partial x-\partial u/\partial y$. That is why SESA2022 counts clockwise circulation as positive. The symbols also change: $\Lambda\to m$ for sources and $\kappa\to\mu$ for doublets.
