# SESA3043 3.6 - Viscous-Inviscid Interaction

> [!abstract] Summary
> A panel method (Chapter 2) gives the inviscid flow round any body, hence $U_e(x)$ and $C_L$, but no drag. A boundary-layer method (MIE, Thwaites) gives $\delta^*(x)$ and $C_f$, hence drag, but needs $U_e(x)$. **Viscous–inviscid interaction (VII)** iterates between the two: panel method → $U_e(x)$ → boundary layer → $\delta^*(x)$ → tell the panel method about the layer → repeat until $\delta^*$ stops changing. There are two ways to tell it:
> - **Surface displacement method (SDM):** move the panels out by $\delta^*$ and re-panel. Direct, but costly.
> - **Surface transpiration method (STM):** keep the original panels and blow through them at
> $$v_s=\frac{\mathrm d(U_e\delta^*)}{\mathrm dx},$$
> which a mass balance shows has the same effect on the outer flow. No re-panelling; this is what XFOIL does.
>
> The coursework uses XFOIL (or XFLR5).

## The topic in one physical picture

The outer flow does not see the real aerofoil. It sees the aerofoil **plus** its boundary layer, thickened by $\delta^*$ everywhere, plus a wake trailing off the trailing edge. That "effective body" is slightly fatter and slightly less cambered at the rear (the upper-surface layer is thicker), so the real aerofoil makes a little less lift than potential theory says, and its pressure no longer fully recovers at the trailing edge, which is pressure (form) drag.

But the layer's thickness depends on the pressure distribution, and the pressure distribution depends on the effective body. Neither can be found first. So guess (no layer), solve, correct, and repeat until the two agree. That loop is VII.

## 1. Why VII is needed (slides 3–8)

- Falkner–Skan, the MIE and Thwaites all give **integral** quantities, and adding $\delta^*$ gives the effective body. That works analytically or with tables for **simple** bodies (the wedge of 3.2). What about **arbitrary** bodies (slide 3)?
- A **panel method** gives the inviscid solution for arbitrary bodies (slide 4; the squirrel). For each panel it gives the tangential velocity $u_i$, which is just $U_e(x)$ at that panel (slides 5–6).
- It is inviscid, so $U_e$ is uniform in $y$ and **only $C_L$** can be obtained.
- In reality a layer forms on the body. A viscous solution applied **inside** the layer gives $C_D$. But where is the boundary between the viscous and inviscid solutions (slide 7)? That is the question VII answers, iteratively (slide 8).

## 2. The VII algorithm (slides 8–9)

1. **Input:** panels, $U_\infty$, $\alpha$.
2. **Inviscid solution** (panel method): $U_e(x)$ on every panel, $C_L$.
3. **Viscous solution** (MIE with a closure, e.g. Thwaites; transition prediction; turbulent closure): $\theta(x)$, $\delta^*(x)$, $C_f(x)$, hence $C_D$.
4. **Convergence check:** has $\delta^*$ (or $v_s$) changed by less than $\varepsilon$ since the last iteration?
5. If not: **recalculate**, re-panel (SDM) or update the blowing (STM), and return to 2.
6. **Output:** final $C_L$, $C_D$.

## 3. Surface displacement method (slides 10–13)

- The viscous solution gives $\delta^*(x)=\int_0^\infty(1-u/U_e)\,\mathrm dy$ at each panel.
- **Convergence:** $\left|\delta^{*(n+1)}-\delta^{*(n)}\right|<\varepsilon$.
- **If not converged: re-panel**, i.e. shift each panel out by $\delta^{*(n)}$ for iteration $n+1$.
- **Drawback** (slide 13): the panel method must be applied to a new body at every iteration (new geometry, new influence matrix). That can be numerically impractical, especially near the trailing edge and in the wake.

## 4. Surface transpiration method (slides 14–23)

### The idea (slides 14–17)

- The displaced panels of SDM must be **streamlines** of the outer flow: the panel method's boundary condition is no flow through a panel.
- STM instead keeps the **original** panels and imposes an imaginary **blowing/suction velocity** $v_s(x)$ through each one, chosen so that the outer streamlines take the same shape as the SDM panels.
- The blowing is not physical, but its effect on the outer flow is the same as re-panelling by $\delta^*$: the effective body is reproduced.

### Deriving v_s (slide 21; revision Q2)

Take a "tunnel" control volume between two stations $x$ and $x+\Delta x$, from the original wall up to the displacement surface (red dashed box in panel b). Flow is incompressible.

**Mass in:**

- through the left face: the equivalent inviscid flow through a gap of height $\delta^*(x)$, i.e. $U_e(x)\,\delta^*(x)$ per unit span;
- through the bottom (the original wall): the blowing, $v_s(x)\,\Delta x$.

**Mass out:**

- through the right face: $U_e(x+\Delta x)\,\delta^*(x+\Delta x)$;
- none through the top, which is the displacement streamline.

**Balance**, $\sum\dot m_{in}=\sum\dot m_{out}$:

$$
U_e(x)\delta^*(x)+v_s(x)\Delta x=U_e(x+\Delta x)\delta^*(x+\Delta x).
$$

Rearrange:

$$
v_s(x)=\frac{U_e(x+\Delta x)\delta^*(x+\Delta x)-U_e(x)\delta^*(x)}{\Delta x}.
$$

Let $\Delta x\to0$:

$$
\boxed{v_s(x)=\frac{\mathrm d\left(U_e\delta^*\right)}{\mathrm dx}.}
$$

> [!tip] Two physical readings
> - The layer's mass-flow **deficit** is $\rho U_e\delta^*$. Where it grows, the layer is pushing more fluid outward; the blowing supplies exactly that outward flux.
> - On a flat plate ($U_e$ constant) $v_s=U_e\,\mathrm d\delta^*/\mathrm dx$, which is the Blasius edge outflow $v_e=0.860\,U_e/\sqrt{Re_x}$ of 1.4. The blowing reproduces the real displacement effect.

### The STM loop (slide 23)

- $v_s$ is easy to specify as a (Dirichlet) normal-velocity boundary condition in the panel code: the same influence matrix, a new right-hand side.
- **Convergence:** $\left|v_s^{(n+1)}-v_s^{(n)}\right|<\varepsilon$; if not, **update $v_s$**.

### XFOIL (slide 24)

XFOIL uses VII with **STM** rather than SDM:

- the viscous solution is an integral (MIE/Thwaites-type) method;
- closures are needed, e.g. Falkner–Skan profiles for laminar layers;
- **transition** is predicted (the $e^n$ method of [[SESA3043 3.4 - Transition to Turbulence|3.4]], with "Ncrit"), and different closures are used for laminar and turbulent layers;
- compressibility corrections extend it up to transonic speeds.

(Further reading: Drela & Giles, 1987.)

### SDM against STM (revision Q1)

| | SDM | STM |
|---|---|---|
| what changes each iteration | the geometry (panels moved by $\delta^*$) | a boundary condition ($v_s$ on each panel) |
| influence matrix | rebuilt every iteration | built once |
| robustness | awkward near the trailing edge and with thick layers or separation | handles the wake and thick layers more easily |
| physical? | yes, the effective body | no, imaginary blowing, but equivalent outer flow |
| used in | conceptual and simple codes | XFOIL, most practical codes |

## 5. Worked example: revision 3.6 Q3, a wind-tunnel test section

> A test section 2 m long, $15\times15$ cm square. $U_0=15$ m/s at the start, where turbulent layers on the walls begin. $\theta/x=0.036\,Re_x^{-1/5}$, $H=9/7$, $\nu=1.46\times10^{-5}$ m²/s. Perform two VII iterations to estimate the centre-line velocity at the exit.

**Model.** The core is inviscid. The four wall layers each displace the core flow inward by $\delta^*$, so the effective area at the exit is $(a-2\delta^*)^2$, $a=0.15$ m (corners ignored). Mass conservation for the core:

$$
U_0a^2=U_e(a-2\delta^*)^2\quad\Longrightarrow\quad U_e=U_0\frac{a^2}{(a-2\delta^*)^2}.
$$

The core speed depends on $\delta^*$; $\delta^*$ depends on $Re_x=U_ex/\nu$, i.e. on the core speed. Hence the iteration.

**Iteration 1** (inviscid guess, $U_e=15$ m/s):

$$
Re_L=\frac{15\times2}{1.46\times10^{-5}}=2.055\times10^6,
\qquad
Re_L^{-1/5}=0.05463,
$$

$$
\theta=0.036\times2\times0.05463=3.934\ \text{mm},
\qquad
\delta^*=\frac97\theta=5.057\ \text{mm},
$$

$$
U_e=15\times\frac{0.15^2}{(0.15-0.010115)^2}=15\times\frac{0.0225}{0.019568}=\boxed{17.25\ \text{m/s}}.
$$

**Iteration 2** ($U_e=17.248$ m/s):

$$
Re_L=2.363\times10^6,\qquad\theta=3.825\ \text{mm},\qquad\delta^*=4.918\ \text{mm},
$$

$$
U_e=15\times\frac{0.0225}{(0.15-0.009836)^2}=\boxed{17.18\ \text{m/s}}.
$$

**Converged value** (iterations 3–5): 17.181 m/s. Two iterations are already within 0.002 m/s: $\delta^*\propto Re^{-1/5}$ depends only weakly on $U_e$, so the loop converges fast. *(All computed.)*

> [!check] Sense of scale
> The wall layers block $1-(0.14016/0.15)^2=12.7\%$ of the exit area, accelerating the core by 14.5%. Real tunnels have slightly diverging walls to compensate, keeping the test-section speed (and static pressure) constant along its length.

> [!info] Where the correlation and $H$ come from
> $\theta/x=0.036Re_x^{-1/5}$ is the 1/7-power-law turbulent result from the MIE, and $H=9/7$ is the 1/7 power law's shape factor ([[SESA2022 T2 - Boundary Layers|SESA2022 T2 §5]]).

## 6. Summary of governing equations (slide 26)

The Chapter 3 table ends with VII: **potential flow plus boundary layer**, coupled. It sits on top of the similarity solution and the MIE (the viscous half) and potential flow (the inviscid half).

**Key formula** (slide 27): blowing/suction velocity in STM, $v_s=\mathrm d(U_e\delta^*)/\mathrm dx$.

## 7. Common mistakes

1. Using $v_s=U_e\,\mathrm d\delta^*/\mathrm dx$ when $U_e$ varies: the product must be differentiated.
2. In the tunnel problem, displacing by $\delta^*$ on only one wall, or by $\theta$ instead of $\delta^*$.
3. Using $U_0$ instead of the updated $U_e$ in $Re_x$ for the second iteration.
4. Claiming STM is physical blowing.
