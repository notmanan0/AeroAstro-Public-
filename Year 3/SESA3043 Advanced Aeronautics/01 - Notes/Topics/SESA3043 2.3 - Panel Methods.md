# SESA3043 2.3 - Panel Methods

> [!abstract] Summary
> Superposition and conformal mapping need the answer to be guessed in advance. A **panel method** does not: it covers any body (the explainer's example is a squirrel) with $N$ straight panels and lets a linear solver find the singularity strengths that make the body a streamline. Three building blocks are derived by integrating point singularities along a panel. A constant-strength **source panel** induces $u=\tfrac{\sigma}{2\pi}\ln(r_1/r_2)$ and $w=\tfrac{\sigma}{2\pi}(\theta_2-\theta_1)$. A **vortex panel** swaps the roles. A **doublet panel** induces the potential $\phi=\tfrac{\mu}{2\pi}(\theta_1-\theta_2)$. On the panel itself these are finite (jumps of $\pm\sigma/2$, $\pm\gamma/2$, $\mp\mu/2$), which removes the point-vortex singularity of the LV method. Two complete methods follow. The **source + vortex** method imposes zero normal velocity (Neumann) at $N$ control points and closes the system with a Kutta condition at the trailing edge, giving $N+1$ equations for $\sigma_1\ldots\sigma_N,\gamma$. The **doublet** method imposes zero total potential inside the body (Dirichlet), adds a wake doublet $\mu_w$ and closes with the Kutta condition $\mu_1-\mu_N-\mu_w=0$. Then $C_L=2\mu_w/(U_\infty c)$ and $C_p=1-q_t^2/U_\infty^2$, with $q_t=-\mathrm d\mu/\mathrm ds$. Validated here against exact cylinder and Kármán–Trefftz solutions.

## The whole topic in one physical picture

Wrap the body in a thin skin and paint singularities on it.

- **Sources** on the skin push fluid outwards: they make the stream **go round** a body of the right shape. They carry no circulation, so no lift.
- **Vortices** on the skin make the fluid **circulate**: they provide the lift.
- **Doublets** do both jobs at once in potential form. A doublet sheet is a jump in $\phi$ across the skin, and its edges behave like vortices.

You do not know in advance how strong the paint must be at each point. So cut the skin into $N$ panels, give each panel an unknown constant strength, and demand that at the middle of each panel the flow does not go through. That is $N$ equations for $N$ unknowns. One more physical requirement is needed to fix the lift: the flow must leave the sharp trailing edge smoothly (Kutta). Solve the linear system once and you have the whole flow.

Everything else is bookkeeping: the geometry of the angles $\theta_1$, $\theta_2$ and the distances $r_1$, $r_2$ from each panel to each control point.

| dimension | 2-D | 2-D or 3-D (quadrilateral panels) |
| loading | point vortices (singular) | distributed over panels (finite) |
| unknowns | $\Gamma_i$ | panel strengths $\sigma_j$, $\gamma$, $\mu_j$ |
| boundary condition | tangency at $3/4$ points | tangency at panel control points |

## 2. The elementary panels (slides 42–48; revision 2.3 Q1)

All three are derived the same way: treat a short element $\delta x_0$ of a panel on $x_1\le x_0\le x_2$ (along the $x$-axis, in the $x$–$z$ plane) as a point singularity, write its effect at $P(x,z)$, and integrate along the panel.

![[aa_panel_elementary_derivation.png|900]]

**Geometry** (`Ch2_Notes_Panel Method.pdf`): for the element at $x_0$, $r^2=(x-x_0)^2+z^2$, $\cos\theta=(x-x_0)/r$, $\sin\theta=z/r$. At the panel ends, $r_k^2=(x-x_k)^2+z^2$ and $\theta_k$ is the angle of $P$ seen from $(x_k,0)$, so that

$$
\tan^{-1}\frac{x-x_k}{z}=\frac\pi2-\theta_k\qquad(k=1,2).
$$

### Source panel (strength σ per unit length)

**Element:** a point source of strength $\sigma\delta x_0$:

$$
\delta q_r=\frac{\sigma\,\delta x_0}{2\pi r},\quad\delta q_\theta=0,\qquad
\delta u=\delta q_r\cos\theta=\frac{\sigma}{2\pi}\frac{x-x_0}{r^2}\delta x_0,\quad
\delta w=\delta q_r\sin\theta=\frac{\sigma}{2\pi}\frac{z}{r^2}\delta x_0.
$$

**Integrate $u$.** Substitute $s=x-x_0$, so $\mathrm ds=-\mathrm dx_0$, and the limits $x_0=x_1,x_2$ become $s=x-x_1,x-x_2$:

$$
u=\frac{\sigma}{2\pi}\int_{x_1}^{x_2}\frac{x-x_0}{(x-x_0)^2+z^2}\mathrm dx_0=-\frac{\sigma}{2\pi}\int_{x-x_1}^{x-x_2}\frac{s\,\mathrm ds}{s^2+z^2}.
$$

With $\int\frac{s\,\mathrm ds}{s^2+z^2}=\tfrac12\ln(s^2+z^2)$ ($z$ is constant in the integral):

$$
u=-\frac{\sigma}{4\pi}\left[\ln r_2^2-\ln r_1^2\right]=-\frac{\sigma}{2\pi}\ln\frac{r_2}{r_1}=\boxed{\frac{\sigma}{2\pi}\ln\frac{r_1}{r_2}}.
$$

**Integrate $w$.** Same substitution, the sign flip reversing the limits:

$$
w=\frac{\sigma}{2\pi}\int_{x-x_2}^{x-x_1}\frac{z\,\mathrm ds}{s^2+z^2}=\frac{\sigma}{2\pi}\left[\tan^{-1}\frac{s}{z}\right]_{x-x_2}^{x-x_1}.
$$

Using the end-angle relation:

$$
w=\frac{\sigma}{2\pi}\left[\left(\frac\pi2-\theta_1\right)-\left(\frac\pi2-\theta_2\right)\right]=\boxed{\frac{\sigma}{2\pi}\left(\theta_2-\theta_1\right)}.
$$

$\theta_2-\theta_1$ is the angle the panel subtends at $P$. A panel seen "edge-on" from far away subtends nothing and induces nothing normal to itself.

### Vortex panel (strength γ per unit length)

**Element:** a point vortex $\gamma\delta x_0$, **clockwise positive** (vorticity along $+y$, into the page; the slide's note):

$$
\delta q_r=0,\quad\delta q_\theta=\frac{\gamma\,\delta x_0}{2\pi r},\qquad\delta u=\delta q_\theta\sin\theta,\quad\delta w=-\delta q_\theta\cos\theta.
$$

These are the source integrands with $u$ and $w$ swapped (and one sign), so the same two integrals give

$$
\boxed{u=\frac{\gamma}{2\pi}(\theta_2-\theta_1),\qquad w=\frac{\gamma}{2\pi}\ln\frac{r_2}{r_1}}.
$$

> [!note] Notes typo (`Ch2_Notes_Panel Method.pdf`)
> The vortex-panel sketch labels the element strength $\delta q_\theta=\dfrac{\mu\,\delta x_0}{2\pi r}$. It should be $\gamma$ (the vortex strength); $\mu$ is the doublet.

### Doublet panel (strength μ per unit length)

**Element:** a general doublet ([[SESA3043 1.3 - Complex Potential, Wedge Flows and Unsteady Bernoulli#5. The general doublet (derivation)|1.3 §5]]) pointing in $+z$, i.e. $\nu=\pi/2$:

$$
\delta\Phi=-\frac{\mu\,\delta x_0}{2\pi(z-z_0)}e^{i\pi/2}=-i\frac{\mu\,\delta x_0}{2\pi(z-z_0)}.
$$

With $z-z_0=re^{i\theta}$: $\delta\Phi=-i\dfrac{\mu\,\delta x_0}{2\pi r}(\cos\theta-i\sin\theta)=-\dfrac{\mu\,\delta x_0}{2\pi r}(\sin\theta+i\cos\theta)$, so

$$
\delta\phi=-\frac{\mu\,\delta x_0}{2\pi r}\sin\theta=-\frac{\mu}{2\pi}\frac{z}{r^2}\delta x_0,\qquad\delta\psi=-\frac{\mu\,\delta x_0}{2\pi r}\cos\theta.
$$

**Integrate** (the same integral as the source's $w$, with a minus sign):

$$
\phi=-\frac{\mu}{2\pi}\int_{x_1}^{x_2}\frac{z\,\mathrm dx_0}{(x-x_0)^2+z^2}=-\frac{\mu}{2\pi}(\theta_2-\theta_1)=\boxed{\frac{\mu}{2\pi}(\theta_1-\theta_2)}.
$$

> [!warning] Slide typo (slide 47)
> The middle line writes $\phi=\dfrac{\sigma}{2\pi}\left[\left(\frac\pi2-\theta_2\right)-\left(\frac\pi2-\theta_1\right)\right]$. It is $\mu$, not $\sigma$. The final boxed result $\phi=\tfrac{\mu}{2\pi}(\theta_1-\theta_2)$ is right.

**Doublet panel = two vortices.** $\theta_1-\theta_2$ is exactly the angle that appears in the potential of a vortex at each end: $\phi_{vortex}=\tfrac{\Gamma}{2\pi}\theta$. So a constant-strength doublet panel is a vortex $+\mu$ at one end and $-\mu$ at the other (slide 47: $\Gamma=\pm\mu$; the slide's "$\pm\mu/2\pi$" is the coefficient in $\phi$). **Net circulation zero**, so doublet panels alone give no lift. The circulation has to come from where adjacent panels do *not* cancel, which is the trailing edge, and the wake.

### Self-induced values (slide 48)

What happens when $P$ is **on** the panel? Let $P$ approach the midpoint from above ($z\to0^+$): $\theta_1\to0$ and $\theta_2\to\pi$. From below: $\theta_1\to0$, $\theta_2\to-\pi$. Also $r_1=r_2$ at the midpoint. So:

| Panel | upper ($+$) | lower ($-$) | Physical meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| source | $u=0$, $w=+\sigma/2$ | $u=0$, $w=-\sigma/2$ | outflow $\sigma/2$ each side: total $\sigma$ |
| vortex | $u=+\gamma/2$, $w=0$ | $u=-\gamma/2$, $w=0$ | tangential jump $\gamma$: a [[Vortex Sheet]] |
| doublet | $\phi=-\mu/2$ | $\phi=+\mu/2$ | potential jump $\mu$ |

**These are finite.** A point vortex at distance zero gives infinity; a panel gives a clean half-strength jump. That is why panel methods are well conditioned where many lumped vortices are not (slide 48).

### Summary table (slide 49)

| Panel | $u$ | $w$ | self-induced |
|---|---|---|---|
| source $\sigma$ | $\frac{\sigma}{2\pi}\ln\frac{r_1}{r_2}$ | $\frac{\sigma}{2\pi}(\theta_2-\theta_1)$ | $u_\pm=0$, $w_\pm=\pm\frac\sigma2$ |
| vortex $\gamma$ | $\frac{\gamma}{2\pi}(\theta_2-\theta_1)$ | $\frac{\gamma}{2\pi}\ln\frac{r_2}{r_1}$ | $u_\pm=\pm\frac\gamma2$, $w_\pm=0$ |
| doublet $\mu$ | potential: $\phi=\frac{\mu}{2\pi}(\theta_1-\theta_2)$ | | $\phi_\pm=\mp\frac\mu2$ |

> [!note] Slide 49 header
> The second column is headed "$u$ velocity" twice; the second is the $w$ velocity. Other options (slide 49): linearly varying strength, or combinations of panel types.

## 3. Setting up a panel method (slide 50; revision 2.3 Q2)

![[aa_panel_method_assembly.png|900]]

1. **Represent the surface by panels.** Straight segments in 2-D, numbered from the trailing edge **clockwise**: lower surface first, then upper. Concentrate panels near the LE and TE, where the flow changes fastest (cosine spacing).
2. **Apply loading** on every panel (sources, doublets, ...).
3. **Add loading that carries circulation** if the chosen panels have none (source and doublet panels have zero net circulation): a vortex distribution, or a wake panel.
4. **Influence coefficients:** the induced velocity (or potential) at every control point from every panel at unit strength.
5. **Flow tangency** at every control point.
6. **Kutta condition** at the TE, to close the system.
7. **Solve** the linear system for all strengths.
8. **Aerodynamic quantities:** $q_t$, $C_p$, $C_L$, $C_M$.

### Panel geometry for the code (explainer pp. 9–13)

For panel $j$ from $(x_{1,j},y_{1,j})$ to $(x_{2,j},y_{2,j})$:

$$
x_{c,j}=\tfrac12(x_{1,j}+x_{2,j}),\quad y_{c,j}=\tfrac12(y_{1,j}+y_{2,j}),\quad l_j=\sqrt{(x_{2,j}-x_{1,j})^2+(y_{2,j}-y_{1,j})^2},\quad\beta_j=\tan^{-1}\frac{y_{2,j}-y_{1,j}}{x_{2,j}-x_{1,j}}.
$$

To use the panel formulas (derived with the panel on the $x$-axis), express control point $i$ in **panel $j$'s local axes**, with origin at the panel midpoint. With $r_{ij}$ and $\Omega=\Psi-\beta_j$ the distance and angle from $(x_{c,j},y_{c,j})$ to $(x_{c,i},y_{c,i})$:

$$
x'_j=r_{ij}\cos(\Psi-\beta_j),\quad y'_j=r_{ij}\sin(\Psi-\beta_j),
$$

$$
r_{1,j}=\sqrt{(x'_j+l_j/2)^2+y_j'^2},\quad r_{2,j}=\sqrt{(x'_j-l_j/2)^2+y_j'^2},\quad\theta_{1,j}=\tan^{-1}\frac{y'_j}{x'_j+l_j/2},\quad\theta_{2,j}=\tan^{-1}\frac{y'_j}{x'_j-l_j/2}.
$$

Use **atan2** for the angles: plain arctan loses the quadrant (the same issue as Euler-angle extraction in [[SESA3047 2.2 - Direction Cosine Matrices and Euler Angles#Why atan2, not arctan (the L6 question)|SESA3047 2.2]]).

> [!note] Coordinates
> The slides derive panels in the $x$–$z$ plane ($w$ = vertical velocity); the explainer and the code use $x$–$y$ ($v$ = vertical). Same algebra.

## 4. Method 1: source + vortex panels (slides 51–53; explainer pp. 8–31)

**Unknowns:** a source strength $\sigma_j$ on each panel, plus **one** vortex strength $\gamma$ shared by all panels (a single extra unknown is enough to set the circulation): $N+1$ in total.

**Induced velocity** at control point $i$ from panel $j$, in panel $j$'s axes:

$$
u'_{ij}=\frac{\sigma_j}{2\pi}\ln\left(\frac{r_1}{r_2}\right)_{ij}+\frac{\gamma}{2\pi}(\theta_2-\theta_1)_{ij},\qquad
w'_{ij}=\frac{\sigma_j}{2\pi}(\theta_2-\theta_1)_{ij}+\frac{\gamma}{2\pi}\ln\left(\frac{r_2}{r_1}\right)_{ij}.
$$

> [!warning] Sign slip (slide 51, and explainer p. 25)
> Both write the vortex contribution to $w'$ as $+\frac{\gamma}{2\pi}\ln(r_1/r_2)$. Slide 45's own vortex-panel result is $w=\frac{\gamma}{2\pi}\ln(r_2/r_1)$, the opposite sign (clockwise $\gamma$: downwash downstream of the panel). Use $\ln(r_2/r_1)$. A quick test: a symmetric aerofoil at $\alpha=0$ must give $\gamma=0$, and at $\alpha>0$ must give $\gamma>0$ (lift up).

**Rotate into panel $i$'s axes** (angle $\beta_j-\beta_i$ between them; explainer p. 14):

$$
u_i=u_j\cos(\beta_j-\beta_i)-v_j\sin(\beta_j-\beta_i),\qquad v_i=u_j\sin(\beta_j-\beta_i)+v_j\cos(\beta_j-\beta_i).
$$

> [!warning] Explainer slip (p. 25, source + vortex)
> The rotation is printed as $u_i=u_j\sin(\beta_j-\beta_i)+v_j\cos(\beta_j-\beta_i)$, $v_i=u_j\cos(\beta_j-\beta_i)-v_j\sin(\beta_j-\beta_i)$: sine and cosine swapped. It must be the same rotation as p. 14 (above). Check: with $\beta_j=\beta_i$ (parallel panels) the components must be unchanged, $u_i=u_j$, $v_i=v_j$. Only the p. 14 form gives that.

**Flow tangency** at each control point (explainer p. 17): the induced normal velocity must cancel the free stream's normal component:

$$
\sum_{j=1}^Na_{ij}\sigma_j+\hat a_i\gamma=U_\infty\sin(\beta_i-\alpha),\qquad i=1\ldots N,
$$

with self-term $a_{ii}=\tfrac12$ (the $\sigma/2$ jump).

**Kutta condition.** $N$ equations, $N+1$ unknowns. Two equivalent options:

1. **Equal tangential speeds** leaving the TE on both sides: $q_{t,1}=q_{t,N}$ (the flow leaves smoothly, and Bernoulli then gives $p_1=p_N$).
2. **An extra control point** $N+1$ just behind the TE, on the bisector of the TE angle, where the velocity **normal to the bisector** is zero (slide 53; explainer pp. 28–31). This forces the flow to leave along the bisector.

**Solve** the $(N+1)\times(N+1)$ system $\mathbf a\,[\sigma_1\ldots\sigma_N,\gamma]^T=\mathbf b$.

**Outputs:**

$$
q_{t,i}=\sum_jq_{t,ij}+U_\infty\cos(\beta_i-\alpha),\qquad C_{p,i}=1-\left(\frac{q_{t,i}}{U_\infty}\right)^2,\qquad\Gamma=\gamma\sum_jl_j,\quad C_L=\frac{2\Gamma}{U_\infty c}.
$$

> [!check] Source panels alone on a cylinder (explainer pp. 22–23)
> Without $\gamma$ there is no circulation and so no lift: the explainer shows a cylinder with $C_p$ matching $1-4\sin^2\theta$ and a symmetric flow field (*"No lift from source panel. What can we do?"*). Panel (a) of the validation figure reproduces this with $N=32$. A useful check: $\sum_j\sigma_jl_j=0$, since a closed body cannot have net outflow.

## 5. Method 2: doublet panels (slides 54–59; explainer pp. 32–42)

**Unknowns:** $\mu_1\ldots\mu_N$ (one per panel) and a **wake doublet** $\mu_w$ on a semi-infinite panel leaving the TE along its bisector: $N+1$.

### Tangency through the potential (revision 2.3 Q3)

Instead of making $\partial\phi/\partial n=0$ on the surface (a condition on velocity), the slides impose a condition on the **potential** (slide 55):

1. If the total flow satisfies $\partial\phi/\partial n=0$ on the closed surface, then **inside** the body (also a region where $\nabla^2\phi=0$) the only solution with that boundary condition is $\phi=$ const. (Uniqueness of the interior Neumann problem.)
2. The constant can be taken as zero.
3. So require the **total** potential to vanish just inside the surface, at each control point: a **Dirichlet** condition, which implies the tangency condition outside.

$$
\sum_{j=1}^{N+1}\phi_{ij}+U_\infty\left(x_i\cos\alpha+y_i\sin\alpha\right)=0,\qquad i=1\ldots N,
$$

where $U_\infty(x\cos\alpha+y\sin\alpha)$ is the free-stream potential, and $\phi_{ij}=a_{ij}\mu_j$ with $a_{ij}=\dfrac{1}{2\pi}(\theta_1-\theta_2)_{ij}$ in panel $j$'s axes. The potential is a **scalar**, so no rotation into panel $i$'s axes is needed (explainer p. 35); this is part of why the doublet method is simpler to code.

**Self-term** $a_{ii}=\tfrac12$: the control point is just **inside** the body, on the side opposite the outward doublet direction, where $\phi=+\mu/2$.

### The Kutta condition for doublets (slide 55; explainer pp. 37–41)

Replace each doublet panel by its two end vortices. At every node **between** two panels, the vortices of the neighbouring panels are $+\mu_j$ and $-\mu_{j+1}$, which leaves a net vortex $\mu_j-\mu_{j+1}$. At the trailing edge, the vortices from panel 1 (lower surface, starting at the TE), panel $N$ (upper surface, ending at the TE) and the wake panel (starting at the TE) meet. The Kutta condition says **no concentrated vortex may remain at the TE** (otherwise the speed there would be infinite):

$$
\boxed{\mu_1-\mu_N-\mu_w=0}\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad\mu_w=\mu_1-\mu_N.
$$

The far end of the wake panel carries the **starting vortex**, at infinity. The wake doublet strength $\mu_w$ is the jump in potential across the wake, which equals the circulation round the aerofoil.

**Matrix:** $N$ rows of tangency plus the Kutta row $[1,0,\ldots,0,-1,-1]$ with right-hand side 0 (slide 56).

### Lift and pressure (slide 56; revision 2.3 Q5)

- **Lift.** Only the wake panel carries net circulation, $\Gamma=\mu_w$:

$$
C_L=\frac{\rho U_\infty\Gamma}{\tfrac12\rho U_\infty^2c}=\boxed{\frac{2\mu_w}{U_\infty c}}.
$$

- **Pressure.** The total potential is zero inside, so just **outside** the surface it equals the jump, $-\mu$. The total tangential velocity is the surface derivative, by finite differences between neighbouring panel centres:

$$
q_{t,i}=-\frac{\mu_i-\mu_{i-1}}{(l_i+l_{i-1})/2},\qquad C_{p,i}=1-\left(\frac{q_{t,i}}{U_\infty}\right)^2.
$$

  No separate free-stream term is added: the Dirichlet formulation makes $\mu$ the **total** potential, not a perturbation.

> [!warning] Slide 42 (explainer) labels lift as $L$
> The boxed equation reads $L=\dfrac{\rho U_\infty\Gamma}{\tfrac12\rho U_\infty^2c}=\dfrac{2\mu_w}{U_\infty c}$. That ratio is the lift **coefficient** $C_L$, not the lift.

### The code, annotated (slides 57–59)

| Block | Code | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| geometry | `xc(i)=0.5*(x(i)+x(i+1))`, `beta(i)=atan2(dy,dx)`, `panlen(i)=sqrt(dx^2+dy^2)` | control points, panel angles $\beta_j$, lengths $l_j$ |
| influence | `x1=xt*cos(beta(j))+yt*sin(beta(j))`, `y1=-xt*sin(beta(j))+yt*cos(beta(j))` | control point $i$ in panel $j$'s axes, origin at panel $j$'s **start** |
| | `a(i,j)=twopiinv*(atan2(y1,x1)-atan2(y1,x1-panlen(j)))` | $a_{ij}=\frac{1}{2\pi}(\theta_1-\theta_2)$ |

The code is "programmed with $\mu$ normalised by $U_\infty$, and $c=1$" (slide 57 handwritten note).

## 6. A worked micro-example: the influence coefficient (suggested)

Panel $j$ from $(0,0)$ to $(1,0)$, control point at $(0.3,0.4)$. In panel axes (already aligned): $x_1=0.3$, $y_1=0.4$.

$$
\theta_1=\mathrm{atan2}(0.4,0.3)=53.13^\circ,\qquad\theta_2=\mathrm{atan2}(0.4,0.3-1)=\mathrm{atan2}(0.4,-0.7)=150.26^\circ.
$$

- Doublet: $a_{ij}=\tfrac{1}{2\pi}(\theta_1-\theta_2)=\tfrac{1}{2\pi}(-1.6952)=-0.2698$.
- Source ($\sigma=1$): $r_1=0.5$, $r_2=0.8062$; $u=\tfrac{1}{2\pi}\ln(0.5/0.8062)=-0.0760$, $w=\tfrac{1}{2\pi}(1.6952)=+0.2698$.

*Verified against direct numerical quadrature of the integrals.* $u<0$ because $P$ is nearer the start of the panel: the flow is pushed back past $x_1$.

> [!warning] Two traps found while validating
> 1. **KT far-field scaling.** With the slide's $2b$ prefactor the circle-plane stream must be $U_{\bar z}=(2/p)U_\infty$ ([[SESA3043 2.1 - Complex Functions and Conformal Mapping#5. The Kármán–Trefftz aerofoil (slides 19–20)|2.1 §5]]). Getting this wrong made the "exact" answer look 6% off.
> 2. **Cusped trailing edges.** On a cambered Joukowski aerofoil (cusped TE) the doublet code converges very slowly and to the wrong value. The two TE panels are nearly coincident, so the inside-the-body control points there are ill-defined. Use a finite TE angle (KT or NACA) for panel methods. This is one reason the coursework uses KT aerofoils.

## 8. Common mistakes

1. Forgetting the self-induced term ($a_{ii}=\tfrac12$): the diagonal is not given by the far-field formula.
2. Using `atan` instead of `atan2` for $\theta_1$, $\theta_2$.
3. Numbering the panels anticlockwise but keeping the code's signs (normals then point inwards, and every sign flips).
4. Adding $U_\infty\cos(\beta-\alpha)$ to $q_t$ in the **doublet** method: $\mu$ is already the total potential.
5. Leaving out the Kutta condition: the system is then singular for the doublet method, or gives zero lift for sources.
6. Expecting drag: inviscid 2-D panel methods give $C_D=0$ (d'Alembert). Real drag needs a boundary-layer model (viscous–inviscid coupling, as in XFOIL).
