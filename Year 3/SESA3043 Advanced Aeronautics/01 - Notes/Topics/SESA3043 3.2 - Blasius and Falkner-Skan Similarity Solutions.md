# SESA3043 3.2 - Blasius and Falkner-Skan Similarity Solutions

> [!abstract] Summary
> Laminar BL profiles at different stations along a flat plate have the **same shape**, just stretched: $u/U_e$ depends on $y$ only through $\eta=y/\xi(x)$, with $\xi=\sqrt{2\nu x/U_e}$. Writing the stream function as $\psi=U_e\xi f(\eta)$ turns the two BL PDEs into one ODE:
> $$\text{Blasius:}\quad f'''+ff''=0,\qquad f(0)=f'(0)=0,\ f'(\infty)=1.$$
> It is solved by **guess-and-shoot** on $f''(0)$, giving $f''(0)=0.4696$. Two integrals of the solution, $\eta^*=\int(1-f')\,\mathrm d\eta=1.217$ ("type A") and $\theta^*=\int f'(1-f')\,\mathrm d\eta=f''(0)=0.4696$ ("type B"), give all the flat-plate results: $\delta_{99}/x=4.91/\sqrt{Re_x}$, $\delta^*/x=1.721/\sqrt{Re_x}$, $\theta/x=C_f=0.664/\sqrt{Re_x}$, $H=2.59$.
>
> The same idea works whenever the edge velocity is a power law, $U_e=cx^m$: the potential flow past a **wedge** of angle $\beta\pi$ (1.3). Then $\xi=\sqrt{2\nu x/((m+1)U_e)}$ and
> $$\text{Falkner–Skan:}\quad f'''+ff''+\beta(1-f'^2)=0,\qquad\beta=\frac{2m}{m+1}.$$
> $\beta>0$ (accelerating flow) gives fuller, thinner profiles; $\beta<0$ (decelerating) gives emptier ones, and at $\beta=-0.19884$ the wall shear vanishes: **separation**.

## The topic in one physical picture

Take velocity profiles at three stations along a flat plate (slide 5). The layer gets thicker downstream, but if you plot each profile against $y/\delta(x)$ they lie on top of one another. The layer at $x=1$ m is the layer at $x=10$ cm, magnified by $\sqrt{10}$ in $y$. Nothing in the flat-plate problem has a length scale of its own (the plate is semi-infinite), so the only way to make $y$ dimensionless is with the diffusion length $\sqrt{\nu x/U_e}$ from 1.4. **Similarity** is the mathematical statement of that: one shape, one ODE, solved once for every station.

Wedge flows keep this property because a power-law $U_e=cx^m$ still has no intrinsic length (scale $x$ by any factor and $U_e$ just scales too). Any other $U_e(x)$, such as a real aerofoil, breaks similarity; that is what the integral methods of 3.3 and 3.5 are for.

## 1. The reduced BL equation in stream-function form (slides 8–11)

From 1.4, for a flat plate ($U_e$ constant, $\mathrm dU_e/\mathrm dx=0$):

$$
\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}+\frac{\partial v}{\partial y}=0,
\qquad
u\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}+v\frac{\partial u}{\partial y}=\nu\frac{\partial^2u}{\partial y^2}.
$$

Introduce $\psi$ with $u=\partial\psi/\partial y$, $v=-\partial\psi/\partial x$. Continuity is then automatic:

$$
\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}+\frac{\partial v}{\partial y}=\frac{\partial^2\psi}{\partial x\partial y}-\frac{\partial^2\psi}{\partial y\partial x}=0.
$$

Substituting into $x$-momentum gives the **five-term** reduced BL equation (Notes Eq. 1):

$$
\underbrace{\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial y}}_{①}\underbrace{\frac{\partial^2\psi}{\partial x\partial y}}_{②}-\underbrace{\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial x}}_{③}\underbrace{\frac{\partial^2\psi}{\partial y^2}}_{④}=\nu\underbrace{\frac{\partial^3\psi}{\partial y^3}}_{⑤}.
$$

## 2. The Blasius similarity variables (slides 12–27; Notes §1)

![[aa_blasius_derivation.png|900]]

**Variable 1**, depends only on $x$:

$$
\xi(x)=\left(\frac{2\nu x}{U_e}\right)^{1/2},
\qquad
\frac{\mathrm d\xi}{\mathrm dx}=\left(\frac{2\nu}{U_e}\right)^{1/2}\frac12x^{-1/2}=\left(\frac{2\nu x}{U_e}\right)^{1/2}\frac{1}{2x}=\frac{\xi}{2x}.
$$

**Variable 2**, the similarity coordinate:

$$
\eta=\frac{y}{\xi},
\qquad
\frac{\partial\eta}{\partial x}=-\frac{y}{\xi^2}\frac{\mathrm d\xi}{\mathrm dx}=-\frac{y}{\xi^2}\frac{\xi}{2x}=-\frac{\eta}{2x},
\qquad
\frac{\partial\eta}{\partial y}=\frac1\xi.
$$

**Variable 3**, the dimensionless stream function, a function of $\eta$ only:

$$
f(\eta)=\frac{\psi}{U_e\xi},
\qquad
\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}=f'\frac{\partial\eta}{\partial x}=-\frac{\eta}{2x}f',
\qquad
\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}=f'\frac{\partial\eta}{\partial y}=\frac1\xi f'.
$$

| No. | Variable | $\partial/\partial x$ | $\partial/\partial y$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | $\xi=(2\nu x/U_e)^{1/2}$ | $\xi/2x$ | 0 |
| 2 | $\eta=y/\xi$ | $-\eta/2x$ | $1/\xi$ |
| 3 | $f=\psi/(U_e\xi)$ | $-\eta f'/2x$ | $f'/\xi$ |

> [!warning] Two scalings of Blasius in the vault
> The course puts $\sqrt2$ inside $\xi$. [[SESA3043 1.4 - Two-Dimensional Incompressible Boundary Layers#5. Check: the exact flat-plate solution (Blasius)|1.4 §5]] and SESA2029 use $\eta_o=y\sqrt{U/\nu x}$, $\psi=\sqrt{\nu Ux}\,f_o$, which gives $f_o'''+\tfrac12f_of_o''=0$ with $f_o''(0)=0.33206$.
> - The two are the same solution: $\eta_o=\sqrt2\,\eta$ and $f_o(\eta_o)=\sqrt2\,f(\eta)$.
> - Hence $f''(0)=\sqrt2\times0.33206=0.4696$, $\eta^*_o=\sqrt2\times1.2168=1.7208$ and $\eta_{99,o}=\sqrt2\times3.472=4.91$.
> - Every **physical** result ($\delta^*/x$, $C_f$, …) is identical. Use the course scaling in this module's exams.

## 3. Deriving the Blasius equation (slides 28–33; Notes §2)

**Step 1: the first derivatives of $\psi=U_e\xi f$.** In $y$ ($\xi$ does not depend on $y$):

$$
\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial y}=U_e\xi\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}=U_e\xi\cdot\frac1\xi f'=U_ef'.
$$

So $f'=u/U_e$: **the slope of $f$ is the velocity profile.** In $x$ (product rule, both $\xi$ and $f$ depend on $x$):

$$
\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial x}=U_e\left(\frac{\mathrm d\xi}{\mathrm dx}f+\xi\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\right)=U_e\left(\frac{\xi}{2x}f-\xi\frac{\eta}{2x}f'\right)=\frac{U_e\xi}{2x}\left(f-\eta f'\right).
$$

**Step 2: the higher derivatives.** Each $y$-derivative brings a factor $1/\xi$:

$$
\frac{\partial^2\psi}{\partial y^2}=\frac{\partial}{\partial y}(U_ef')=U_ef''\frac1\xi=\frac{U_e}{\xi}f'',
\qquad
\frac{\partial^3\psi}{\partial y^3}=\frac{U_e}{\xi}f'''\frac1\xi=\frac{U_e}{\xi^2}f''',
$$

$$
\frac{\partial^2\psi}{\partial x\partial y}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x}(U_ef')=U_ef''\frac{\partial\eta}{\partial x}=-\frac{U_e\eta}{2x}f''.
$$

**Step 3: substitute into ①②−③④=ν⑤.**

$$
U_ef'\left(-\frac{U_e\eta}{2x}f''\right)-\frac{U_e\xi}{2x}\left(f-\eta f'\right)\frac{U_e}{\xi}f''=\nu\frac{U_e}{\xi^2}f'''.
$$

The $\xi$ cancels in the second term:

$$
-\frac{U_e^2}{2x}\eta f'f''-\frac{U_e^2}{2x}\left(f-\eta f'\right)f''=\nu\frac{U_e}{\xi^2}f'''.
$$

**Step 4: tidy up.** Multiply by $\dfrac{2x}{U_e^2}$:

$$
-\eta f'f''-ff''+\eta f'f''=\frac{2\nu x}{U_e\,\xi^2}f'''.
$$

By definition $\xi^2=2\nu x/U_e$, so the coefficient on the right is exactly 1, and the $\eta f'f''$ terms cancel:

$$
\boxed{f'''+ff''=0}\qquad\text{(Notes Eq. 7)}
$$

**Boundary conditions:**

| Physical condition | In $\psi$, $u$ | In $f$ |
|---|---|---|
| the wall is a streamline | $\psi(y=0)=0$ | $f(0)=0$ |
| no slip | $u(y=0)=0$ | $f'(0)=0$ |
| edge | $u\to U_e$ as $y\to\infty$ | $f'(\infty)=1$ |

> [!tip] Why it worked
> $x$ and $y$ appear only through $\eta$ once the $\eta f'f''$ terms cancel. That cancellation is not luck: it happens **because** $\xi\propto\sqrt x$, the scaling found by order of magnitude in 1.4. Any other power of $x$ would leave an explicit $x$ behind.

## 4. Solving it: guess-and-shoot (slides 34–38)

Three conditions are needed at one end to march a third-order ODE, but only two are known at the wall ($f$, $f'$); the third is at infinity. So (panel d):

1. guess $s=f''(0)$;
2. integrate $f'''=-ff''$ from $\eta=0$ as an initial-value problem (e.g. RK4, as in [[SESA2029 A6 - Higher-Order Time Integration, Runge-Kutta and the Blasius Equation|SESA2029 A6]]);
3. check $f'$ at large $\eta$: too big → reduce $s$; too small → increase it;
4. repeat (bisection or secant) until $f'(\infty)=1$.

Result: $f''(0)=0.46960$ (course scaling; computed here with RK45 and Brent's method, to five figures).

## 5. Blasius boundary-layer properties (slides 39–74; Notes §3)

![[aa_falkner_skan_solutions.png|900]]

### Thickness (slide 42)

$f'=0.99$ at $\eta_{99}=y_{99}/\xi$. The slides read $\eta_{99}=3.5$ off the plot; the computed value is 3.47. Then

$$
\delta_{99}=\eta_{99}\,\xi=\eta_{99}\sqrt{\frac{2\nu x}{U_e}}=\eta_{99}\sqrt2\,\frac{x}{\sqrt{Re_x}}.
$$

$$
\boxed{\frac{\delta_{99}}{x}=\frac{4.91}{\sqrt{Re_x}}}\quad(\text{slides, with }\eta_{99}=3.5:\ 4.95\approx5.0).
$$

### Integral type A (slide 45; Notes Eq. 9)

For large $\eta$, $f'\to1$ and $f$ grows linearly: $f\to\eta-1.217$ (the dashed asymptote in panel e of the Blasius figure).

$$
\eta^*\equiv\int_0^\infty(1-f')\,\mathrm d\eta=\big[\eta-f\big]_0^\infty=\lim_{\eta\to\infty}\left[\eta-(\eta-1.217)\right]-\left[0-f(0)\right]=1.217.
$$

The displacement thickness follows by changing variable, $\mathrm dy=\xi\,\mathrm d\eta$:

$$
\delta^*=\int_0^\infty\left(1-\frac{u}{U_e}\right)\mathrm dy=\xi\int_0^\infty(1-f')\,\mathrm d\eta=\xi\eta^*=1.217\sqrt{\frac{2\nu x}{U_e}},
\qquad
\boxed{\frac{\delta^*}{x}=\frac{1.721}{\sqrt{Re_x}}.}
$$

### Integral type B (slides 46–68; Notes Eqs. 10–11)

$$
\theta^*\equiv\int_0^\infty f'(1-f')\,\mathrm d\eta.
$$

**Integrate by parts**, with $u=(1-f')$ and $\mathrm dv=f'\,\mathrm d\eta$, so $v=f$ and $\mathrm du=-f''\,\mathrm d\eta$:

$$
\theta^*=\big[f(1-f')\big]_0^\infty+\int_0^\infty ff''\,\mathrm d\eta.
$$

**Use the Blasius equation**, $ff''=-f'''$:

$$
\theta^*=\big[f(1-f')\big]_0^\infty-\int_0^\infty f'''\,\mathrm d\eta=\big[f(1-f')\big]_0^\infty-\big[f''\big]_0^\infty.
$$

**Evaluate the limits.**

- At $\eta=0$: $f(0)=0$, so $f(1-f')=0$.
- As $\eta\to\infty$: $f\sim\eta$ grows but $1-f'$ decays faster than any power (slide 59 writes $1-f'=c_1/\eta^2+\dots$ to make the point; it actually decays like a Gaussian). So $f(1-f')\to0$.
- $f''(\infty)=0$ (the shear dies out).

$$
\theta^*=0-0-\left[0-f''(0)\right]=f''(0)=0.4696.
$$

The momentum integral **equals the wall shear parameter**: no extra integration is needed. Then

$$
\theta=\xi\theta^*=0.4696\sqrt{\frac{2\nu x}{U_e}},
\qquad
\boxed{\frac{\theta}{x}=\frac{0.664}{\sqrt{Re_x}}},
\qquad
\boxed{H=\frac{\eta^*}{\theta^*}=\frac{1.217}{0.4696}=2.59.}
$$

### Skin friction (slide 72; Notes Eq. 15)

$$
C_f=\frac{\tau_w}{\tfrac12\rho U_e^2}=\frac{2\mu}{\rho U_e^2}\left.\frac{\partial u}{\partial y}\right|_{0}
=\frac{2\nu}{U_e^2}\,U_ef''(0)\frac{\partial\eta}{\partial y}
=\frac{2\nu}{U_e\xi}f''(0)
=\frac{2\nu}{U_e}\left(\frac{U_e}{2\nu x}\right)^{1/2}f''(0).
$$

Simplify: $\dfrac{2\nu}{U_e}\sqrt{\dfrac{U_e}{2\nu x}}=\sqrt{\dfrac{2\nu}{U_ex}}=\dfrac{\sqrt2}{\sqrt{Re_x}}$, so

$$
\boxed{C_f=\frac{\sqrt2\times0.4696}{\sqrt{Re_x}}=\frac{0.664}{\sqrt{Re_x}}.}
$$

> [!check] The drag link from 3.1
> $\dfrac{\mathrm d\theta}{\mathrm dx}=\dfrac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dx}\left(0.664\,x^{1/2}\sqrt{\nu/U_e}\right)=\dfrac{0.664}{2}\sqrt{\dfrac{\nu}{U_ex}}=\dfrac{0.332}{\sqrt{Re_x}}=\dfrac{C_f}{2}$ ✓ (Notes Eq. 16). The numbers agree because $\theta^*=f''(0)$: the same 0.4696 appears in both.

## 6. Generalised laminar BLs: Falkner–Skan (slides 75–96; Notes)

![[aa_falkner_skan_derivation.png|900]]

### Where U_e = cx^m comes from

The 1.3 potential $\Phi=-kz^{m+1}$ is the flow over a wedge of total angle $\beta\pi$ with $\beta=2m/(m+1)$, and its wall speed is a power law in the distance from the apex, $U_e=cx^m$ ([[Wedge and Corner Flows]], [[SESA3043 1.3 - Complex Potential, Wedge Flows and Unsteady Bernoulli|1.3]]). Then

$$
\frac{\mathrm dU_e}{\mathrm dx}=cmx^{m-1},\qquad\frac{1}{U_e}\frac{\mathrm dU_e}{\mathrm dx}=\frac{m}{x}.
$$

The pressure-gradient term $U_e\,\mathrm dU_e/\mathrm dx$ is now non-zero, so the reduced BL equation has **six** terms (slide 89):

$$
\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial y}\frac{\partial^2\psi}{\partial x\partial y}-\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial x}\frac{\partial^2\psi}{\partial y^2}=\nu\frac{\partial^3\psi}{\partial y^3}+U_e\frac{\mathrm dU_e}{\mathrm dx}.
$$

### Step 1: variables

$$
\xi=\left[\frac{2\nu x}{(m+1)U_e}\right]^{1/2},\qquad\eta=\frac y\xi,\qquad f=\frac{\psi}{U_e\xi}.
$$

Since $U_e\propto x^m$, $\xi\propto x^{(1-m)/2}$. Differentiate $\xi$ with the product rule:

$$
\frac{\mathrm d\xi}{\mathrm dx}=\left(\frac{2\nu}{m+1}\right)^{1/2}\frac12\left[(xU_e)^{-1/2}-x^{1/2}U_e^{-3/2}\frac{\mathrm dU_e}{\mathrm dx}\right]
=\left(\frac{2\nu}{m+1}\right)^{1/2}\frac12\left(\frac{x}{U_e}\right)^{1/2}\left[\frac1x-\frac mx\right]
=\frac{\xi}{2x}(1-m).
$$

Then

$$
\frac{\partial\eta}{\partial x}=-\frac{y}{\xi^2}\frac{\xi}{2x}(1-m)=\frac{\eta}{2x}(m-1),
\qquad\frac{\partial\eta}{\partial y}=\frac1\xi,
$$

$$
\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}=\frac{\eta}{2x}(m-1)f',\qquad\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}=\frac1\xi f'.
$$

(With $m=0$ these reduce to the Blasius table ✓.)

### Step 2: derivatives of ψ = U_e ξ f

As before, $\psi_y=U_ef'$, $\psi_{yy}=\dfrac{U_e}{\xi}f''$, $\psi_{yyy}=\dfrac{U_e}{\xi^2}f'''$ ($\xi$ and $U_e$ do not depend on $y$).

Now **three** factors depend on $x$:

$$
\psi_x=\frac{\mathrm dU_e}{\mathrm dx}\xi f+U_e\frac{\mathrm d\xi}{\mathrm dx}f+U_e\xi\frac{\partial\eta}{\partial x}f'
=U_e\left[\frac mx\xi f+\frac{\xi}{2x}(1-m)f+\frac{\xi\eta}{2x}(m-1)f'\right].
$$

Collect over $\dfrac{U_e\xi}{2x}$: $2m+(1-m)=m+1$, so

$$
\psi_x=\frac{U_e\xi}{2x}\left[(m+1)f+(m-1)\eta f'\right].
$$

Differentiate in $y$ (each $\partial/\partial y$ of a function of $\eta$ gives $1/\xi$; $\partial(\eta f')/\partial y=(f'+\eta f'')/\xi$):

$$
\psi_{xy}=\frac{U_e\xi}{2x}\cdot\frac1\xi\left[(m+1)f'+(m-1)(f'+\eta f'')\right]=\frac{U_e}{2x}\left[2mf'+(m-1)\eta f''\right].
$$

### Step 3: substitute

$$
U_ef'\cdot\frac{U_e}{2x}\left[2mf'+(m-1)\eta f''\right]-\frac{U_e\xi}{2x}\left[(m+1)f+(m-1)\eta f'\right]\frac{U_e}{\xi}f''=\nu\frac{U_e}{\xi^2}f'''+U_e\cdot\frac{U_em}{x}.
$$

### Step 4: simplify

Multiply by $\dfrac{2x}{U_e^2}$:

$$
2mf'^2+(m-1)\eta f'f''-(m+1)ff''-(m-1)\eta f'f''=\frac{2\nu x}{U_e\xi^2}f'''+2m.
$$

The $(m-1)\eta f'f''$ terms cancel. From the definition of $\xi$, $\dfrac{2\nu x}{U_e\xi^2}=m+1$:

$$
2mf'^2-(m+1)ff''=(m+1)f'''+2m.
$$

Rearrange:

$$
(m+1)f'''+(m+1)ff''+2m\left(1-f'^2\right)=0.
$$

### Step 5: divide by (m+1)

$$
f'''+ff''+\frac{2m}{m+1}\left(1-f'^2\right)=0,
\qquad
\boxed{f'''+ff''+\beta\left(1-f'^2\right)=0,\quad\beta=\frac{2m}{m+1}.}
$$

Same boundary conditions as Blasius; solved by guess-and-shoot. $\beta=0$ recovers Blasius.

> [!warning] Typo in `Ch3_Notes_Falkner-Skan.pdf`
> The last two lines of the derivation (p. 3), including the **boxed Eq. 8**, are printed as $f'''+f'f''+\beta(1-f'^2)=0$. The line above them, $(m+1)f'''+(m+1)ff''+\dots$, and slide 94 both have $ff''$, which is correct. With $f'f''$ the equation would not reduce to Blasius at $\beta=0$.

### Step 6: thicknesses and friction for any β

With $\mathrm dy=\xi\,\mathrm d\eta$, exactly as for Blasius:

$$
\delta^*=\xi\eta^*(\beta),\qquad\theta=\xi\theta^*(\beta),\qquad C_f=\frac{2\nu}{U_e\xi}f''(0;\beta).
$$

The $\theta^*=f''(0)$ shortcut does **not** carry over: integrating by parts now leaves a $\beta\int(1-f'^2)$ term. Read $\eta^*$ and $\theta^*$ from the table (slide 96). Since $\xi=x\sqrt{2/(m+1)}/\sqrt{Re_x}$ with $Re_x=U_e(x)x/\nu$:

$$
\frac{\delta^*}{x}=\frac{\eta^*\sqrt{2/(m+1)}}{\sqrt{Re_x}},\qquad
\frac{\theta}{x}=\frac{\theta^*\sqrt{2/(m+1)}}{\sqrt{Re_x}},\qquad
C_f\sqrt{Re_x}=2f''(0)\sqrt{\frac{m+1}{2}}.
$$

> [!tip] Physical trends
> - **Accelerating** ($\beta>0$): larger wall shear, thinner layer, lower $H$. The favourable pressure gradient pushes the slow near-wall fluid along.
> - **Decelerating** ($\beta<0$): the wall shear falls; at $\beta=-0.19884$, a wall that turns **away** from the flow by only $0.0994\pi\approx18^\circ$, $\partial u/\partial y|_0=0$ and the layer is on the point of separating. A laminar layer tolerates only a **very mild** adverse gradient.

### The effective body (slides 97–99)

Knowing $\beta$, Falkner–Skan gives $\eta^*$ and hence $\delta^*(x)=\eta^*\xi(x)$. Adding $\delta^*$ to the wedge gives the **apparent body** that the outer potential flow actually sees. Viscous solution inside, inviscid outside: the idea developed into [[SESA3043 3.6 - Viscous-Inviscid Interaction|3.6]] for arbitrary bodies.

## 7. Worked examples

### 7.1 Slides 103–106: δ99 near the front stagnation point of a cylinder

$U_\infty=30$ m/s, $R=0.1$ m, $\nu=1.46\times10^{-5}$ m²/s.

1. **Edge velocity from potential flow** (1.3): on the cylinder surface $U_e=2U_\infty\sin\theta$. Near the stagnation point, with $x=R\theta$ measured round the surface, $\sin\theta\approx\theta\approx x/R$:
$$U_e\approx\frac{2U_\infty}{R}x\quad\Rightarrow\quad c=\frac{2U_\infty}{R}=600\ \text{s}^{-1},\quad m=1.$$
2. **Falkner–Skan parameter:** $\beta=\dfrac{2(1)}{1+1}=1$ (stagnation-point flow, Hiemenz).
3. **Edge of the layer:** from the $\beta=1$ column of the table, $f'=0.99$ at $\eta_{99}\approx2.4$ (computed: 2.38).
4. **Thickness:**
$$\delta_{99}=\eta_{99}\xi=\eta_{99}\sqrt{\frac{2\nu x}{(m+1)U_e}}=\eta_{99}\sqrt{\frac{2\nu x}{2\cdot2U_\infty x/R}}=\eta_{99}\sqrt{\frac{\nu R}{2U_\infty}}.$$
$$\sqrt{\frac{1.46\times10^{-5}\times0.1}{60}}=1.560\times10^{-4}\ \text{m},\qquad\delta_{99}=2.4\times0.156=\boxed{0.374\ \text{mm}}\ \ (\text{exact }\eta_{99}:\ 0.371\ \text{mm}).$$

> [!tip] The layer has constant thickness at a stagnation point
> $x$ cancelled: for $m=1$, $\xi=\sqrt{\nu/c}$ does not depend on $x$. Flow arrives along the stagnation streamline and is spread sideways as fast as viscosity thickens it, so the layer starts with a finite thickness, unlike the flat plate's $\delta=0$ at the leading edge. Also $\delta^*=0.648\xi=0.101$ mm and $\theta=0.292\xi=0.046$ mm.

### 7.2 Revision 3.2 Q4: the wedge with β = 0.3

$m=\beta/(2-\beta)=0.3/1.7=0.1765$ (a $54^\circ$ wedge). From the table: $\eta^*=0.9110$, $\theta^*=0.3857$. $\sqrt{2/(m+1)}=\sqrt{2/1.1765}=1.3038$:

$$
\frac{\delta^*}{x}=\frac{0.9110\times1.3038}{\sqrt{Re_x}}=\frac{1.188}{\sqrt{Re_x}},\qquad
\frac{\theta}{x}=\frac{0.3857\times1.3038}{\sqrt{Re_x}}=\frac{0.503}{\sqrt{Re_x}},\qquad H=2.36.
$$

**As a function of distance along the wall:** $Re_x=cx^{m+1}/\nu$, so

$$
\delta^*=1.188\sqrt{\frac{\nu}{c}}\,x^{(1-m)/2}=1.188\sqrt{\frac{\nu}{c}}\,x^{0.412},\qquad\theta=0.503\sqrt{\frac{\nu}{c}}\,x^{0.412}.
$$

The layer grows more slowly than on a flat plate ($x^{0.41}$ against $x^{0.5}$) because the accelerating flow keeps thinning it.

> [!check] Numbers, for a feel
> Take $U_e=10$ m/s at $x=1$ m ($c=10$) and $\nu=1.5\times10^{-5}$: at $x=0.1$, 0.5 and 1 m, $\delta^*=0.56$, 1.09 and 1.45 mm and $\theta=0.24$, 0.46 and 0.62 mm. *(Computed.)*

## 8. Common mistakes

1. Mixing the two Blasius scalings: $f''(0)=0.332$ belongs to $f'''+\tfrac12ff''=0$; this module uses 0.4696.
2. Forgetting $(m+1)$ inside $\xi$ for Falkner–Skan, which changes every thickness by $\sqrt{2/(m+1)}$.
3. Using $\theta^*=f''(0)$ for $\beta\neq0$: it holds only for Blasius.
4. Taking $Re_x$ with $U_\infty$ instead of the local $U_e(x)$.
5. Confusing $\beta$ (wedge angle $/\pi$) with $m$ (velocity exponent); $\beta=2m/(m+1)$.
