# Passive vs Active Thermal Control

Passive thermal control keeps a spacecraft within its temperature limits with surface finishes, insulation, radiators and conductive paths, using no power and no moving parts; active control adds powered means such as pumped fluid loops and controlled heat transport.

The passive strategy starts by wrapping most of the spacecraft in multi-layer insulation, then sizes radiating surfaces to keep equipment below its upper limits in the hottest phase and heaters to keep it above its lower limits in the coldest. The finishes are chosen for their $\alpha_S/\varepsilon$. The lecture's analogy is the polar bear: insulating fur, with dark radiating patches for shedding heat. Passive control is simple, reliable, light and cheap, and every spacecraft uses it.

Active control costs power, mass, money and the reliability of mechanisms, but it is flexible and moves heat at much higher rates, so it is added only when the environment swings too widely or a component's tolerance is too tight for passive means. The thermal engineer works with almost every other subsystem, because every heat source and every tolerance on board is part of the balance.
