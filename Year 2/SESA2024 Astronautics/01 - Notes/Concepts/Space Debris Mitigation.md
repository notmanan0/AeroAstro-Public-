# Space Debris Mitigation

Space debris is every non-functional human-made object in Earth orbit or re-entering, from dead satellites and rocket bodies to fragments and paint flakes; mitigation is the set of measures that limit how much is created, and remediation removes what is already there.

About 36 500 objects are tracked, with about a million larger than 1 cm and some 12 400 t in orbit. Most fragments come from a few break-ups at 700–1000 km. A break-up sends fragments into orbits through the same point but with different periods, so the cloud shears into a ring along the orbit.

The IADC's four measures are to limit debris released in operations, minimise break-ups (passivate tanks and batteries), dispose of spacecraft after the mission, and prevent collisions; the UN's seven guidelines add avoiding intentional destruction and limiting long-term presence in LEO and GEO. Disposal in LEO used to be required within 25 years, and since 2022 within 5 years for FCC-licensed satellites. The cheapest method is one retrograde burn that lowers the perigee so drag finishes the job (about 136 m/s from 800 km to a 300 km perigee); a drag sail can do it without propulsion; GEO satellites move to a graveyard orbit about 300 km higher.

Even with full compliance, collisions keep the LEO population growing (the Kessler syndrome), so active debris removal is needed too: RemoveDebris, ClearSpace, Astroscale's ELSA-M and the UK's COSMIC.
