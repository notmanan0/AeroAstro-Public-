# Space Debris Mitigation

## Definition

> [!note] Definition
> **Space debris** (IADC): *all human-made objects, including fragments and elements thereof, in Earth orbit or re-entering the atmosphere, that are non-functional.*
>
> **Mitigation** (IADC):
> 1. limit debris released in normal operations;
> 2. minimise on-orbit break-ups;
> 3. **post-mission disposal**;
> 4. prevent collisions.

## Explanation
- Population (ESA 2024): about 36 500 tracked; about 1 million larger than 1 cm; about 130 million larger than 1 mm; about 12 400 t in orbit.
- **UN guidelines (7)** add: avoid intentional destruction; minimise stored-energy break-ups (passivation); limit long-term presence in LEO; limit interference with GEO (graveyard orbit).
- **Disposal rule**: less than 25 years in LEO. The **FCC's 5-year rule** was adopted on 29 September 2022.
- **Cheapest disposal**: a single retrograde burn lowers the perigee while the apogee stays put. Drag then shrinks $a$ and $e$ until re-entry. Drag sails are an alternative ($\delta a\propto S$).
- **Remediation**: even with compliance, collisions make the LEO population grow (the Kessler cascade). Active debris removal is needed: Astroscale ELSA-M, ClearSpace, RemoveDebris, UK COSMIC.
- **Fragment clouds** spread along-track because different ΔVs give different periods (Kepler 3). Forward-kicked fragments trail behind; rear-kicked ones lead.

## Examples
- 2021/22 A6: why fragments spread rapidly in the orbit plane (5 marks).
- 2022/23 A6: place S, A and B on an apogee–period (Gabbard) plot.
- 2019/20 B3(iii): a drag sail (9 m² against 1 m²) gives a 9× faster decay.
