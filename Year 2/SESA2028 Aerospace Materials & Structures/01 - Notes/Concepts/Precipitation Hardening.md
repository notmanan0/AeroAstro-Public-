# Precipitation Hardening

Precipitation hardening strengthens an alloy with a fine dispersion of second-phase particles, produced by dissolving the alloying element at high temperature, quenching to trap it in supersaturated solution, and ageing at a moderate temperature so that it precipitates finely.

Small coherent precipitates, whose atomic planes continue those of the matrix, are cut by dislocations, but only at extra stress because they are harder. Large incoherent ones behave like internal grain boundaries: dislocations cannot cut them, so they are pinned at the particles and bow out between them. Either way the strength depends mainly on how closely the particles are spaced, so the aim is many fine particles rather than a few coarse ones of the same total volume.

In an Al–Cu alloy the three steps are solution treatment in the single-phase $\alpha$ field, a water quench that leaves the copper supersaturated in $\alpha$, and ageing at around 120–190 °C in the $\alpha+\theta$ field, where metastable $\theta'$ nucleates everywhere. Strength against ageing time then rises to a peak and falls. Under-aged, precipitates are still forming; at peak age (the T6 temper) the spacing is optimal; over-aged, small particles dissolve to feed large ones because a fixed volume of precipitate has interface area $3V/r$, so coarsening lowers the interface energy, and the fewer, more widely spaced particles strengthen less. T4 is the naturally aged equivalent.

![[materials_al_cu_heat_treatment.png|800]]

The same treatment applies to the 2xxx (Cu), 6xxx (Mg–Si), 7xxx (Zn–Mg) and Al–Li alloys, to $\beta$ titanium alloys, and, with the coherent $\gamma'$ phase, to nickel superalloys ([[Gamma Prime Strengthening]]). Its weak points are temperature, since precipitates keep coarsening if the part runs hot, and welding, which redissolves or coarsens them in the heat-affected zone. Precipitate-free zones along grain boundaries, where boundary diffusion has drained the solute into coarse boundary particles, are soft and affect both toughness and corrosion.
