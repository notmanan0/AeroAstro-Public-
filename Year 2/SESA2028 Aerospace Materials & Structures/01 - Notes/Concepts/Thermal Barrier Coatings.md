# Thermal Barrier Coatings

A thermal barrier coating is a layered ceramic coating on an internally cooled superalloy component, mainly a turbine blade, that insulates the metal so it runs far cooler than the gas around it.

The system has three layers on the single-crystal superalloy. An intermetallic bond coat eases the step between the metal's thermal expansion and the ceramic's, which matters because the blade is heated and cooled every flight. A thermally grown oxide is deliberately formed on the bond coat, oxidising the bond coat rather than the superalloy, and acts as the oxidation barrier. On top goes a zirconia ceramic layer about 100–400 μm thick, deposited by electron-beam physical vapour deposition or by spraying. Being an insulator, the ceramic carries a steep temperature drop, and cooling air inside the blade keeps the metal cooler still.

![[materials_tbc_temperature_profile.png|750]]

Coatings in general are either diffusion coatings, where the coating element interdiffuses with the substrate, as aluminium into nickel to form an aluminide, or overlay coatings deposited as a separate layer. Vapour deposition gives thin, controlled layers but is slow, costly and hard to control in composition. Plasma and high-velocity oxy-fuel spraying fire partly molten powder that splats onto the surface like mud on a wall: quick, cheap and versatile, but line-of-sight, porous and rough, so the substrate is pre-roughened to key the coating in. Ceramics are seldom used as large structural parts because they are brittle and flaw-sensitive, so coating is their main structural role at high temperature, the cheaper alternative to a [[MMC vs CMC|ceramic matrix composite]] part.
