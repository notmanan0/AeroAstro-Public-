# MMC vs CMC

| | Metal matrix composite (MMC) | Ceramic matrix composite (CMC) |
|---|---|---|
| Examples | $\mathrm{Al\text{-}SiC_p}$ (brake discs), Ti-SiC$_f$ (bling), Al-B$_f$ | SiC/SiC (turbine shrouds, blades), C/C (brakes, nozzles), $\mathrm{Al_2O_3/Al_2O_3}$ |
| Why reinforce | **specific stiffness**, wear resistance, tailored thermal expansion and conductivity | **toughen** a brittle matrix against its intrinsic flaws |
| Matrix | ductile, tough; Al/Ti/Mg to about 300-800 °C, Co/Ni hotter | brittle, porous after sintering; stable above 1000 °C |
| Interface wanted | strong (load transfer) | **weak**, so fibres debond and pull out |
| Toughening | matrix plasticity | fibre bridging, crack deflection and splitting, pull-out |
| Oxidation | depends on matrix (Ti forms a protective $\mathrm{TiO_2}$) | interfaces are oxidation paths, so worse than a monolithic ceramic; C/C needs coatings |
| Manufacture | powder metallurgy, foil-fibre-foil diffusion bonding, stir or squeeze casting, spray deposition; **fibre-matrix reactions** at temperature | slurry infiltration + sintering, CVI, polymer infiltration and pyrolysis (repeated 4-10×) |
| Cost | high | highest |
| Uptake | niche; mostly ground transport, electronics | niche, high value: hot aero-engine sections, rocket nozzles |

## Exam angles

- **"Advantages of CMC over MMC"** (2016-17 A1(i)): higher temperature capability, lower density, higher stiffness, oxidation and creep resistance. Disadvantages: low toughness even when reinforced, compression-only strength data, expensive slow processing, porosity, inspection and certification difficulty.
- **"Why is matrix SiC weaker than fibre SiC?"** (2017-18 A1(ii)): the matrix is made by sintering or infiltration, leaving **porosity, larger grains and flaws**. Fibres are made by controlled deposition as thin, near-defect-free filaments. Strength is flaw controlled (Griffith), and a smaller volume has fewer critical flaws (Weibull).
- **Ti MMC vs C-based CMC for a hot application** (2024-25 MQ2(b)): the Ti MMC has specific strength, damage tolerance and a proven manufacturing route but is limited to about 600 °C by oxidation. C/C and C-SiC go far hotter at low density but oxidise without coatings and have low toughness.
