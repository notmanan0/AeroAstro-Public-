# Localised Corrosion

Localised corrosion is attack concentrated on a small anode by a local difference in electrode potential, caused either by a different material or by a different concentration, usually of oxygen; it can be up to $10^5$ times faster than uniform corrosion.

The speed comes from the area ratio. The corrosion current is usually limited by the cathodic reaction, so it grows with the cathode's area, while Faraday's law makes the penetration rate proportional to the current divided by the anode's area. A large cathode feeding a small anode is therefore the worst combination, and every form of localised attack is a version of it.

In galvanic corrosion two different metals in contact through an electrolyte form a cell and the more active one corrodes, as a zinc fastener does in steel. The same effect protects galvanised steel: where the zinc coating is scratched, the exposed steel is the cathode and the zinc around it corrodes instead. Pitting is galvanic corrosion inside one alloy. A second-phase particle that is anodic to the matrix dissolves, fed by the whole surrounding surface acting as cathode, and the pit develops its own aggressive chemistry. Pits take many shapes, deep and narrow, undercut, or running along grain boundaries, and each is a stress concentration that can start a fatigue crack.

Crevice corrosion is driven by oxygen instead of a second metal. Water trapped under a bolt head, a gasket or debris starts with the same oxygen as the water outside, but corrosion uses it up and little more can diffuse in. The de-aerated crevice becomes the anode and the open surface a large cathode; corrosion products clog the gap and the trapped solution turns acidic, so the attack accelerates out of sight. The rust ring under a water drop on steel is the same mechanism in miniature.

Prevention follows from the mechanism: avoid dissimilar metals in contact, avoid crevices and water traps, prefer welded to bolted joints where gaps would form, keep surfaces clean, and never pair a small anode with a large cathode ([[Corrosion Protection]]).
