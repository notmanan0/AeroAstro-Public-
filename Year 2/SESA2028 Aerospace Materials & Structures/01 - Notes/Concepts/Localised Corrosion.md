# Localised Corrosion

Localised corrosion can be up to about $10^5$ times faster than uniform corrosion. The key ideas:

- a **potential difference** (different metals, or different concentrations of ions or $\mathrm O_2$);
- the **cathode controls the rate** (its area limits the total current);
- a **small anode** concentrates that current, giving deep, fast attack.

$$
\text{penetration rate}\propto i_{anode}=\frac{I}{A_{anode}},\qquad I\propto A_{cathode}.
$$

## Galvanic

Dissimilar metals + electrical contact + electrolyte.

- Bad: Zn-plated fasteners in steel (small Zn anode, lost quickly); Al skin riveted with Cu-bearing or steel fasteners.
- Good: galvanised steel, where the Zn coating protects scratched steel sacrificially.

## Pitting

A locally active spot surrounded by a large cathode. In Al alloys pits form at second-phase particles: an anodic particle dissolves, or a cathodic particle makes the surrounding matrix anodic. Pit shapes include narrow-deep, wide-shallow, undercut and grain-boundary following. **A pit is a stress concentration, so a fatigue origin.**

## Crevice and differential aeration

Under bolt heads, washers, laps and debris. $\mathrm O_2$ is used up in the stagnant gap and cannot be replaced, so the **gap becomes the anode** and the open surface a **large cathode**. Corrosion products seal the gap and the chemistry acidifies: an **autocatalytic**, hidden process. A water droplet on steel is the same mechanism: the aerated rim is the cathode, the starved centre the anode, and a rust ring forms between them.

## Intergranular

Preferential grain-boundary attack, e.g. [[Sensitisation and Weld Decay]] in stainless steel, or precipitate-free zones in Al alloys.

## Flow-assisted

- **Cavitation**: collapsing bubbles strip protective films (propellers, pumps).
- **Erosion-corrosion**: particles remove films; the effect depends on impact angle ($90^\circ$ deforms or chips; shallow angles cut).

## Control

Avoid small-anode/large-cathode couples, crevices and water traps; insulate dissimilar metals; paint the **cathode** (or both), never only the anode.
