# Composite Manufacturing Routes

With composites the **material and the component are made at the same time**. The process therefore fixes the $V_f$, void content, fibre orientation and cost.

| Route | Wet/dry | Tooling | Voids and quality | Speed and cost | Shapes and uses |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Hand lay-up** | wet | open 1-part mould; gel coat | high voids, resin-rich areas, skill dependent | slow, cheap tooling | hulls, one-offs |
| **Spray-up / chopped mat** | wet | open | poor, random fibres | cheap | panels, tanks |
| **Pultrusion** | wet | heated die, pulled | good | fast, continuous, automated | constant section only: rods, tubes, I-beams |
| **Filament winding** | wet or dry | rotating mandrel | high $V_f$, low voids | fast | bodies of revolution: pressure vessels, COPVs, tubes. Mandrel removal is a problem; wind angle fixed within a pass |
| **Resin transfer moulding (RTM)** | dry preform + injection | closed 2-part | high quality | high mould cost, needs volume | fairly large parts, dinghy hulls |
| **Compression moulding** | dry | heated closed 2-part | good consolidation, low voids | more costly than a 1-part bag mould | helicopter and wind-turbine blades |
| **Vacuum bag + autoclave** | dry (prepreg) | 1-part + bag, peel ply, breather | **< 1 % voids**, best | expensive: frozen prepreg, autoclave capital, size limit | aerospace primary structure |
| **Resin film infusion (RFI)** | film + dry fabric | out-of-autoclave | high | avoids prepreg cost and autoclave size | A380 rear pressure bulkhead |

## Why vacuum bagging beats hand lay-up (2013-14 B2(iv))

Pressure removes trapped air and excess resin, giving uniform high $V_f$, low voids, repeatable properties and no dependence on operator skill. It is limited by the cost of prepreg and autoclaves, autoclave size, and slow cycle times.

## Common defects

Voids; resin-rich (low $V_f$) regions; poor fibre-matrix interface (sizing); fibre misalignment or waviness; residual stress and warping from unsymmetric lay-ups.
