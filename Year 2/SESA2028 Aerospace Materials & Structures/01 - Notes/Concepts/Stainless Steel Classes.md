# Stainless Steel Classes

Stainless steels contain **> 12 % Cr**, which forms a continuous, coherent, adherent, self-healing $\mathrm{Cr_2O_3}$ passive film. It blocks both ion and electron transport. **Ni** stabilises austenite (FCC) down to room temperature and below.

| Class | Typical composition | Structure | Pros | Cons | Uses |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Austenitic** (304 = 18/8, 316 + Mo, 316L low C) | ≥ 16 % Cr, ≥ 6 % Ni | FCC | best corrosion; **no DBT** (cryogenic); non-magnetic; formable; weldable (L grades); high work hardening | chloride **SCC**; sensitisation; cannot be hardened by heat treatment | ~70 % of all stainless: food, chemical, cryogenic, satellites |
| **Ferritic** (430, 409) | 10.5-18 % Cr, low C | BCC | cheap; **best SCC resistance**; magnetic | DBT; poor formability and weldability; not heat-treatable; moderate corrosion | exhausts, trim, appliances |
| **Martensitic** (410, 416, 420, 431, 440) | ~12-17 % Cr + higher C (431: + 2 % Ni) | Q&T martensite | hardenable, strong, wear resistant | lower corrosion; brittle if untempered | cutlery, surgical tools, valves, **steam-turbine LP blades** |
| **Duplex** (2304, 2205) | 22-23 % Cr, 4-5 % Ni + N, Mo | $\gamma+\alpha$ | ~2× annealed austenitic strength; SCC resistance near ferritic; pitting better than 316 | brittle below about -50 °C and after exposure above 300 °C | pipelines, offshore, chemical tanks |
| **Precipitation hardening** (17-4PH) | Cr-Ni + Cu, Al, Nb | martensite or austenite + precipitates | high strength + corrosion | cost | aerospace fittings, shafts |

## Roles of the key elements (a common exam part)

- **Cr**: passive film (> 12 %); a ferrite stabiliser; a carbide former, which causes sensitisation.
- **Ni**: austenite stabiliser (FCC: tough, no DBT, formable); lowers $M_s$; helps passivity in reducing acids.
- **Mo**: pitting and crevice resistance (316).
- **Low C / Ti / Nb**: prevent sensitisation ([[Sensitisation and Weld Decay]]).
- **N**: austenite stabiliser and strengthener.

## Tempered martensitic stainless for IGT low-pressure blades (2022-23 MQ2(iv))

Q&T gives high strength and fatigue resistance. About 12 % Cr gives adequate corrosion and erosion resistance in wet steam at 200-300 °C. It is cheaper than Ti or Ni alloys and machinable. Mo and V give tempering resistance and creep strength.
