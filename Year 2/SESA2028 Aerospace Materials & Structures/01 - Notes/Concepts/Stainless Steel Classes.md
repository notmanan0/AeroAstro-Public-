# Stainless Steel Classes

Stainless steels are iron alloys with more than about 12 % chromium, enough for a protective chromium oxide to form in preference to other oxides, and they are classed by their matrix as austenitic, ferritic, martensitic or duplex, mainly according to their nickel and carbon.

With enough chromium, a thin, coherent $\mathrm{Cr_2O_3}$ film forms and blocks the ion and electron transport that further oxidation needs; with less, a mixed oxide forms that does not protect. Harry Brearley found the effect in Sheffield in 1913 when some of his gun-barrel steels refused to rust. Chromium sets the corrosion resistance; nickel, an austenite stabiliser, decides whether the FCC phase survives to room temperature.

![[materials_stainless_cr_ni_map.png|750]]

Austenitic grades, at least 16 % Cr and 6 % Ni, such as 304 (18/8) and the low-carbon, molybdenum-bearing 316L, make up about 70 % of use. Being FCC they have no ductile–brittle transition, so they suit cryogenic and space applications, are largely non-magnetic, work-harden strongly and resist corrosion best, but they are prone to stress corrosion cracking and to weld decay if carbon is not kept low ([[Sensitisation and Weld Decay]]). Ferritic grades, 10.5–18 % Cr with low carbon (430, 409), are BCC, cheaper, less formable and not heat-treatable, but resist stress corrosion cracking well. Martensitic grades have about 12 % Cr with more carbon (410, 420, 440) and are quenched and tempered for hardness; 431 reaches 16 % Cr and stays martensitic because its 2 % nickel keeps austenite stable to a low enough temperature to quench. Duplex grades, high in chromium with some nickel, mix austenite and ferrite: much stronger, with stress corrosion resistance between the two and corrosion resistance like austenitic, but usable only between about −50 °C, where the ferrite turns brittle, and 300 °C; pipelines are a typical use.

Past papers ask for the roles of chromium and nickel, for a stainless versus martensitic carbon steel comparison, and for the problems of welding austenitic grades.
