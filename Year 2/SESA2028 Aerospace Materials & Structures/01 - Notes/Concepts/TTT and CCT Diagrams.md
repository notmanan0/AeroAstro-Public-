# TTT and CCT Diagrams

A time–temperature–transformation (TTT) diagram shows, for one steel composition, how long austenite held at each temperature takes to start and finish transforming, and a continuous cooling transformation (CCT) diagram shows the same for steady cooling at fixed rates.

The phase diagram says what is stable but not how long it takes to form, so neither question about a cooling rate can be answered from it. A TTT diagram is built by quenching samples to a hold temperature and measuring the fraction transformed against time; for eutectoid steel at 705 °C pearlite starts after about 5.8 minutes and finishes at about 67. Joining the start and finish times at many temperatures gives two C-shaped curves on a log-time axis. They have a nose because just below 727 °C the driving force to nucleate is small, while far below it the atoms lack the thermal energy to diffuse; the transformation is fastest in between, at about 540 °C and 1 s for plain eutectoid steel. Coarse pearlite forms just below the eutectoid temperature, fine pearlite near the nose, bainite below it, and the horizontal $M_s$ and $M_f$ lines mark martensite.

![[materials_ttt_critical_cooling_rate.png|750]]

Real parts cool continuously, which a TTT diagram can only mimic as a staircase of holds, so CCT diagrams are measured at fixed cooling rates and sit a little lower and to the right. A furnace cool (annealing) gives coarse pearlite, an air cool (normalising) fine pearlite, an oil quench perhaps pearlite, bainite and some martensite, and a water quench faster than the critical cooling rate, the slowest that just misses the noses, gives martensite. Alloying elements and a coarser austenite grain size shift the curves to the right, lowering the critical rate and raising [[Hardenability and Jominy Test|hardenability]].
