# Kutta-Joukowski Theorem

The lift per unit span on any two-dimensional closed body in a steady, inviscid, incompressible stream is $L' = \rho_\infty V_\infty\Gamma$, where $\Gamma = -\oint\mathbf V\cdot d\mathbf s$ is the circulation round the body (clockwise positive in this course). The drag is zero.

The shape of the body matters only through the circulation it supports, and for an aerofoil that circulation is fixed by the [[Kutta Condition]]. For the cylinder the theorem can be checked directly: integrating $-p\sin\theta$ over the surface, only the cross term $4k\sin\theta$ in $(2\sin\theta+k)^2$ survives, giving $\rho V_\infty\Gamma$ ([[SESA2022 Exam 2016-17 Solutions]] Q1(ii); also [[SESA2022 Exam 2015-16 Solutions]] Q2). In thin aerofoil theory $\Gamma = \int_0^c\gamma(\xi)\,d\xi$, so $c_l = 2\Gamma/(V_\infty c) = \pi(2A_0+A_1)$. In lifting-line theory it holds section by section, $L'(y) = \rho V_\infty\Gamma(y)$, so $L = \rho V_\infty\int\Gamma\,dy$.

Physically, circulation speeds the flow up over the top of the body and slows it underneath, and Bernoulli turns that velocity difference into a pressure difference. The Magnus effect on a spinning ball is the same result, with the circulation generated by viscosity.
