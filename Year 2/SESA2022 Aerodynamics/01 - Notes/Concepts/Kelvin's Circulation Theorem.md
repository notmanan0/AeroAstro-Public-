# Kelvin's Circulation Theorem

Kelvin's theorem says that in an inviscid, barotropic flow with conservative body forces, the circulation round a closed material curve, one that moves with the fluid, stays constant: $D\Gamma/Dt = 0$.

Its best-known consequence is the starting vortex. Before an aerofoil moves, a large curve enclosing it and the surrounding air has $\Gamma = 0$. After start-up the aerofoil carries a bound circulation $\Gamma$, and since the total must still be zero, a starting vortex of strength $-\Gamma$ must have been shed from the trailing edge and left behind in the wake. The same happens on every change of lift, from a gust, a flap deflection or a pitch change: each sheds a matching vortex into the wake, which is the basis of unsteady aerodynamics (the Wagner and Theodorsen functions). The theorem also means that irrotational flow stays irrotational, which justifies using potential flow everywhere outside the boundary layers and wakes where vorticity is generated. Its three-dimensional counterparts are Helmholtz's vortex theorems ([[Biot-Savart Law and Helmholtz Theorems]]).

[[SESA2022 Exam 2020-21 Solutions]] Part C uses a vortex near the ground and a wall as the starting vortex of a take-off.
