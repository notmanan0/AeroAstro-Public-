# Compressor Stall and Surge

A compressor **stalls** when the boundary layers on its blades separate on a large scale at high positive incidence, at low flow or high speed, and the pressure rise is lost. In rotating stall the stalled cells travel round the annulus. **Surge** is a system-wide instability in which the whole compressor flow oscillates violently and can even reverse.

Compressors are prone to it because turning the flow back towards axial diffuses it, an adverse pressure gradient, and the suction-surface boundary layer thickens and separates if the incidence or loading is too high. Turbines, with accelerating flow, are much more robust.

Throttling a compressor at constant speed shows the sequence (2021-22 Q4(vi)). Restricting the outlet reduces the mass flow and $V_x$, so $\phi$ falls and the incidence rises. At first the pressure ratio rises towards the peak of the speed line while the efficiency passes its peak. Then the blades separate, giving rotating stall, a drop in pressure ratio and efficiency, noise and vibration. Finally the compressor surges, with large oscillations of pressure and mass flow, possible flow reversal and flame-out.

The standard ways of avoiding it (2015-16 Q1(iii), 2018-19 Q1(iv)) are:

- multi-spool layouts, so that each compressor runs near its design speed;
- variable inlet guide vanes and stators to reset the incidence at part speed;
- handling or bleed valves to dump flow at start-up;
- lower stage loading, with more stages or blades;
- casing treatment;
- an adequate surge margin on the working line;
- careful fuel scheduling during accelerations.
